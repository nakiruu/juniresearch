# 11 — How professional rating frameworks work, and where Juniper stands

Research date 2026-09-22. A methodology-level survey of the frameworks the industry actually uses,
a factor-family gap matrix against the built system, a concrete proposal to make uncertainty *widen
the rating bands* (the one structural idea Juniper lacks), and a fair account of what Juniper does
that the professionals do not.

## Executive summary

1. Every mature framework is a **composite of several independent models** — Morningstar (fair value
   × uncertainty × moat), StarMine (eight alpha models in a static linear combination), Zacks
   (four revision factors). Juniper's `decide()` is architecturally in this family already.
2. The single biggest structural difference: **professionals let uncertainty change the rating
   thresholds.** Morningstar demands a 20 % discount for 5 stars at Low uncertainty and far more at
   High/Very High; Juniper's `desk.json` thresholds are one fixed set, and its conviction score is a
   separate number that never moves the bands.
3. Juniper has **no expectations-dynamics model at all** (Zacks is *only* that; StarMine's ARM is its
   top-weighted input) — the largest factor-family gap (9.md).
4. Juniper's label is **absolute** (expected-return thresholds); sell-side and StarMine ranks are
   largely **relative** to a universe. For a 21-name book absolute is right; the cross-sectional
   composite should be surfaced as a relative rank *alongside* it, not instead.
5. **Credit-rating logic is the closest analogue to 6.md**: an anchor from a business-risk ×
   financial-risk matrix, then non-compensatory modifiers and caps. Juniper's gates/modulators are
   that pattern; the missing piece is the anchor's *uncertainty dimension*.
6. Professionals **maintain** ratings continuously (price moves change E daily; "under review"
   states; target-price refresh). Juniper's reports are static per filing.
7. Juniper does four things the pros generally do not: an explicit **bear floor**, **one-way
   non-compensatory caps**, **per-filing grounding** of every figure, and a **mechanical reverse-DCF
   discipline on the analyst's E**. Keep all four.
8. Adopt next, in order: uncertainty-widened bands (data exists), an expectations sleeve (snapshot
   history), rating maintenance (live-price E), then relative rank display.

## 1. Survey

### Morningstar
Fair value estimate from a DCF, then a **Star Rating = price / fair value, banded by an Uncertainty
Rating** (Low, Medium, High, Very High, Extreme). "The higher the Uncertainty Rating, the greater the
margin of safety we demand": at Low uncertainty a 20 % discount earns 5 stars and a 25 % premium
earns 1 star; the cut-offs widen with each tier and were set from the historical performance of the
uncertainty rating plus option-pricing on implied volatility ([Morningstar intro](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/an-introduction-morningstar-uncertainty-rating),
[methodology PDF](https://www.morningstar.com/content/dam/marketing/shared/research/methodology/705988Morningstar_Equity_Research_Methodology.pdf),
[enhanced uncertainty rating](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/everything-you-need-know-about-enhanced-morningstar-uncertainty-rating)).
Uncertainty is driven by the *range of outcomes* — sales/margin predictability, operating and
financial leverage, cyclicality, event risk. Separate Economic Moat (five sources) with a Moat Trend,
and a Capital Allocation rating (balance sheet, investment, shareholder distributions). Juniper's
3.md moat and 8.md's capital-allocation factors map directly; the uncertainty banding does not exist.

### Zacks Rank
Four inputs, all from estimate revisions — Agreement, Magnitude (60-day consensus change, current and
next FY), Upside (most-accurate vs consensus), Surprise — recomputed nightly into a 1-5 rank
([Zacks Rank guide](https://advisortools.zacks.com/Content/ZAT_ZacksRankGuide.pdf)). Style Scores add
Value/Growth/Momentum. Nothing about intrinsic value, moat or balance sheet: it is a pure
expectations-dynamics engine, and Juniper has none of its inputs (9.md).

### StarMine (LSEG)
Sixteen models; the **Combined Alpha Model** is "an optimal, static, linear combination" of Analyst
Revisions, Relative Valuation, Intrinsic Valuation, Price Momentum, Earnings Quality, Smart Holdings,
Insider Filings (US) and Short Interest (US), with regional weights
([LSEG CAM](https://www.lseg.com/en/data-catalogue/analytics/quantitative-analytics/starmine-combined-alpha-model),
[brochure](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/data-analytics/en_us/documents/brochures/starmine-quantitative-analytics-brochure.pdf)).
ARM uses SmartEstimates (accuracy-weighted analysts) and recommendation changes. StarMine is the
closest professional analogue to `decide()`: Juniper has Relative Valuation (composite Value),
Intrinsic Valuation (4.md), a weak Price Momentum, and part of Earnings Quality (accruals); it lacks
ARM, Smart Holdings, Insider Filings and Short Interest entirely. Note that CAM is *compensatory*
(linear) — 6.md's non-compensatory gates are a deliberate departure, appropriate for a desk that
publishes a label rather than a ranked portfolio.

### Sell-side
Buy/Neutral/Sell defined either as absolute 12-month total-return bands (~+15 % / ±15 % / −15 %) or
relative to the analyst's coverage or a sector index; rating distributions are disclosed under FINRA
2241; targets carry a 12-month horizon and are refreshed at prints and events; "conviction lists"
overlay a higher bar. Consensus targets are systematically optimistic (9.md §2).

### Buy-side scorecards and quant classics
Quality-Value-Growth-Momentum-Catalyst grids; Greenblatt (earnings yield + ROIC), Piotroski (in
Juniper's gates), Graham screens; expected-value framing with position sizing by conviction (Kelly
fractions, expected shortfall). The common thread: the *rating* and the *size* are separate
decisions — Juniper conflates neither, but has no sizing output.

### Credit ratings as an analogue
S&P's corporate methodology anchors on a business-risk × financial-risk matrix, then applies
modifiers (diversification, capital structure, liquidity, governance, comparable analysis) and
caps (liquidity can cap the rating regardless of the anchor). Non-compensatory, matrix-anchored,
modifier-adjusted — 6.md's architecture almost exactly. The transferable lesson is the **liquidity
and governance modifiers as caps**, and the anchor's explicit *volatility of profitability* term.

## 3. Uncertainty should widen the bands — a concrete proposal

Juniper's `conviction` is a separate 0-100 number; the label thresholds never move. Morningstar's
insight is that the *same* expected upside deserves a different label at different uncertainty.
Proposal — an **uncertainty tier** computed from data already in the pack, and tier-indexed
thresholds in `desk.json`:

**Tier inputs (0-3 points each, sum → tier):** analyst-target dispersion (`(hi−lo)/median` > 0.6 =
2, > 1.0 = 3); leverage (net debtor with ND/EBITDA > 3 = 2); cyclicality (SIC semis/miners/energy = 2;
utilities/staples = 0); data completeness (< 5 fiscal years, or any layer abstained = 1 each);
concentration HHI > 0.25 = 1; event/overhang flags = 1-3; realized vol once 252-day history exists
(top tercile = 2). **Low ≤ 2, Medium 3-5, High 6-8, Very High ≥ 9.**

**Tier-indexed thresholds (illustrative, calibrate on the book):**

| Tier | STRONG BUY E ≥ | BUY E ≥ | SELL E ≤ | STRONG SELL E ≤ | Bear floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 20 % | 10 % | −5 % | −20 % | 15 % |
| Medium | 25 % | 12 % | −7 % | −25 % | 15 % |
| High | 35 % | 18 % | −10 % | −30 % | 20 % |
| Very High | 50 % | 25 % | −15 % | −40 % | 25 % |

`desk.json` shape: `rating.byUncertainty: { low: {...}, medium: {...}, high: {...}, veryHigh: {...} }`
with today's numbers as `low`. `deriveLabel(c, cfg.byUncertainty[tier])` is a one-line change;
`validate-judgment` reads the same table. The conviction *score* remains as the explanatory
companion (6.md §4) — the tier is what moves the bands.

**On the book:** AMD (dispersion 1.58 → 3, semis → 2, one abstention → 1, HHI 0.36 → 1 = **7, High**)
would need E ≥ 18 % for BUY — it has +12 %, so HOLD stands *for a stated reason*. NVDA (tight
dispersion, semis, full data ≈ **3-4, Medium**) keeps BUY at +23 %. NEE (utility, leverage 2 →
**Medium**) keeps BUY. CRWV (3 years, distressed, young issuer → **Very High**) would require +25 %
for BUY and a 25 % bear floor — consistent with the gate's SELL ceiling from a different direction.

## 4. Relative vs absolute

Keep the absolute label — a desk publishing standalone reports owes the reader an expected-return
statement, not a rank. But the cross-sectional composite (5.md) already yields a relative position;
surface it as **"quality/value rank: 71st percentile of the book"** on the RatingBlock, and once the
universe exceeds ~50 names, add a quintile overlay so a BUY in the bottom quintile is visibly a
weaker BUY. Never let the relative rank change the absolute label (the one-way invariant).

## 5. Rating maintenance

Professionals refresh: at every print, at events, and continuously as price moves E. Juniper's E
is computed once from the filing-date price. Proposal:
- On every page render, recompute E/D/R from the **stored scenarios × live `quote.price`**; if
  `deriveLabel` differs from the published label, show a **"rating drift"** chip (no silent change).
- **Under review** state for merger-arb / pending events (the WBS precedent) — suppress the label.
- Hard refresh at the next filing; soft refresh (targets/scenarios) at a print if drift persists.
- Persist `rating.asOfPrice` so drift is auditable.

## 6. What Juniper does that the professionals do not

- **Explicit bear floor** (15 % below price) — Morningstar has no equivalent; sell-side bears are
  often formalities. Keep, and make it tier-indexed (§3).
- **One-way, non-compensatory caps** — CAM is linear; sell-side ratings are judgment. This is the
  credit-rating discipline applied to equities, and it is right for a published label.
- **Per-filing grounding** of every figure and the editorial gate — no vendor framework audits
  its own prose this way.
- **Mechanical reverse-DCF as a discipline on the analyst's E** — StarMine's IV model is similar in
  spirit but is a rank input, not a check on a human's scenarios.

## 7. Worked comparison — AMD vs NVDA

| | Juniper today | Morningstar-style | Zacks-style | StarMine-style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AMD | HOLD; E +12 %, R 0.37; moat Narrow/Widening; MoS −60 %; composite 71 | High uncertainty → needs ~30 %+ discount to FV for 4-5 stars; at a −60 % MoS it is 1-2 stars (Sell/Underperform) | cannot compute (no revision history); the year's upward revisions would likely rank it 1-2 (Buy) | RV low, IV low, EQ fine, PM high → mid-rank; short interest modest |
| NVDA | BUY; E +23 %, R 0.78; WIDE/Stable; MoS −14 %; composite 81 | Medium uncertainty → near fair value → 3 stars (Hold) | strong revisions → 1 (Strong Buy) | RV mid, IV mid, PM high, EQ good → upper rank |

The frameworks disagree most on AMD: the *valuation* lenses (Morningstar, Juniper intrinsic) are
negative; the *expectations* lenses (Zacks, StarMine ARM) are positive. Juniper's HOLD is the
reconciliation — but it reaches it without ever computing the expectations side, which is why the
conviction should be marked low (it is: 75/high today, which the audit flags as too generous).

## 8. What to adopt next (ranked by value / effort)

1. **Uncertainty tier + tier-indexed thresholds** (§3) — all inputs exist except realized vol; a
   `desk.json` table and a one-line `deriveLabel` change. *Structural upgrade, small code.*
2. **Expectations sleeve** via analysts/estimates snapshots (9.md) — the largest factor gap.
3. **Rating maintenance**: live-price E with a drift chip and an under-review state (§5).
4. **Relative rank display** from the composite (§4) — UI only.
5. **StarMine-style positioning inputs** (short interest, insider) as conviction modifiers (9.md).
6. **Credit-style liquidity cap** as a gate input (10.md §5).
