# 10 — Missing factors III: risk overlays — concentration, regime, cyclicality, regulatory, liquidity, events, calibration

Research date 2026-09-22. The built layers judge *quality* and *value*; none of them judges the
*shape of the risk* around the rating. This document specifies the overlays a rating should carry,
what each does to the bear floor or the conviction/uncertainty tier, and which are free today.

## Executive summary

1. **Concentration is the cheapest unbuilt factor in the system.** `segments` and `geoMix` are in
   every pack and scored by nothing; a Herfindahl index over each is ten lines and directly deepens
   the bear floor for single-segment or single-region names (CBRS, UEC, the China-exposed semis).
2. **Customer concentration** — the disclosure that matters most for small suppliers (KTOS, UEC) —
   is tagged in XBRL as `ConcentrationRiskPercentage1` with a `MajorCustomersAxis`; it is
   dimensional, so it needs the free source's inline-XBRL parser, not `companyfacts`.
3. **The macro layer is static** (rf 4.3 %, ERP 4.5 %, sector β). A live 10-year yield and a monthly
   ERP are keyless; the bigger win is making *bear cases* regime-conditional (deeper in credit-stress
   regimes), not timing factors — the factor-timing evidence is weak and should not be over-claimed.
4. **Cyclicality is the intrinsic layer's largest hazard**: trailing FCF anchors a DCF at peak or
   trough. Mid-cycle normalisation (5-year average margins × current revenue) and a "peak-margin"
   detector are computable now; a longer history would allow a Shiller-style 7-10-year average.
5. **Regulatory / legal / political overhang** (V interchange, LLY drug pricing, NVDA/AMD export
   controls, NEE rate cases) lives in the risk-factor text already in the pack. A grounded
   LLM-extracted `overhang` flag with a quoted anchor is the right mechanism; ESG *scores* are not
   (return evidence is mixed at best).
6. **Liquidity, float and size** should set *confidence and position size*, not the label: a $10
   UEC and a $4 T NVDA can carry the same label with very different certainty.
7. **Event & tail risk** — debt maturity walls, warrant overhangs (AMD's ~20 %), binary catalysts,
   merger-arb states (WBS) — deserve a small typed `events[]` block that deepens the bear floor or
   suspends the rating ("under review").
8. **Scenario probabilities (25/50/25) are uncalibrated.** Base rates of ≥15 % / ≥30 % one-year
   drawdowns by volatility bucket, and an implied-vol sanity check on the bear, would turn the
   15 % desk floor from a constant into a function of the name's own risk.

## 1. Concentration (free today)

**Segment HHI** `H_seg = Σ share_i²` over `segments.items`; **geographic HHI** over `geoMix.items`.
Thresholds (industry-standard DOJ scale, reused): < 0.15 diversified, 0.15-0.25 moderate, > 0.25
concentrated; a single reportable segment is H = 1.

**Customer concentration.** US GAAP requires disclosure of customers ≥ 10 % of revenue. XBRL tags it
as `ConcentrationRiskPercentage1` with `ConcentrationRiskByBenchmarkAxis` (revenue / receivables) ×
`ConcentrationRiskByTypeAxis` (customer / supplier / geographic) and optionally `MajorCustomersAxis`
([XBRL US DQC 0109](https://xbrl.us/data-rule/dqc_0109/)). Because it is dimensional it is absent from
`companyfacts`; `lib/facts/free/filing-xbrl.ts` already parses the filing's inline XBRL for
class-scoped EPS and can pull these members the same way. Supplier concentration (single foundry,
single fab) uses the same tag with the supplier type member.

**Evidence.** Customer-concentrated suppliers show higher return volatility, lower profitability
persistence and larger drawdowns on the loss of a major customer (Patatoukas 2012 finds mixed
average-return effects but clear risk effects). Geographic concentration in politically exposed
regions (China/Taiwan for semis) is a tail-risk driver the desk has already narrated by hand.

**Placement.** A **bear-floor modulator**: `D_floor += 0.05` when H_seg > 0.25 *or* top customer
≥ 20 % *or* one region > 50 % outside North America; and an **uncertainty-tier** input (11.md). Not a
gate and not a label driver — concentration is a risk shape, not a verdict.

**Worked (from the packs).** AMD: three product segments, Data Center ≈ 48 % → H_seg ≈ 0.36
(concentrated, and rising); five regions with the largest ≈ 30 % → H_geo ≈ 0.2 (moderate). NEE: FPL
vs NEER ≈ 60/40 → H ≈ 0.52 (concentrated by construction — regulated utilities are, and should not
be penalised for it: sector caveat). A money-center bank: segments are business lines, H ≈ 0.3; geo
is domestic → the modulator should be sector-gated like the rest.

## 2. Macro sensitivity & regime

| Exposure | Proxy computable now | Better data | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rate / duration | implied growth from 4.md (higher → longer duration) | 400-d history → beta to TLT | bear deepening when the 10-y rises |
| FX | non-US share of `geoMix` | — | uncertainty tier |
| Commodity | SIC 1000-1499 (miners), 2900s (energy) | spot series | UEC: bear from a commodity drawdown, not a DCF |
| Credit regime | `interestCoverage`, net debtor flag | HY OAS (FRED, keyless) | deeper distress tiers when spreads > 500 bp |
| Inflation pass-through | gross-margin stability (3.md test D) | — | quality read |

**Live macro.** rf = FRED `DGS10` (keyless CSV); ERP = Damodaran's monthly implied ERP (public page).
Both belong in `desk.json` with an `asOf`, refreshed by a `desk:macro` script; the audit (I3) already
asks for one source. Moving rf by 100 bp moves every WACC ~1 pt and every reverse-DCF implied growth
~2-3 pts — publish the sensitivity rather than pretend precision.

**Regime.** Factor-timing evidence is weak (Asness et al. 2017; "factor timing is deceptively
difficult"); the defensible use of regime is **conditional bear sizing**: when HY spreads or the VIX
sit in their top quintile, raise the desk bear floor from 15 % to 20-25 % for net debtors. Encode as
a desk policy, not a model.

## 3. Cyclicality & normalisation

- **Mid-cycle earnings:** `normFCF = revenue_t × mean(FCF margin, 5 yr)`; use as an *alternative* OE₀
  in `intrinsic.ts` and publish both (the trailing anchor is 4.md's admitted hazard; on AMD FY23-25
  the difference is large).
- **Peak-margin detector:** `operatingMargin_t / max(5-yr)` > 0.95 with a rising 3-yr slope → flag
  "at peak"; for semis/miners this should suppress STRONG BUY corroboration.
- **Backlog / book-to-bill** (KTOS, defense, semicap): `RevenueRemainingPerformanceObligation`
  (XBRL) and MD&A text — the desk's KTOS run already narrated backlog by hand.
- **Inventory cycle** (semis): `InventoryNet` growth vs revenue growth; DSI expansion precedes
  downturns (1.md §15 wanted it).
- Longer term: a Shiller-style 7-10-year average needs `companyfacts` history the free source
  already downloads — only the pack's 5-year window truncates it.

## 4. Regulatory, legal, political & ESG

The pack's `context.riskFactorsExcerpt` and MD&A carry the overhangs the desk has hand-narrated:
interchange regulation (V), drug-pricing (LLY), export controls (NVDA/AMD), rate cases (NEE), antitrust
(ICE/CME as venues). Mechanise as a **grounded LLM extraction** — the same lint-checked, quote-anchored
pass the report prose already goes through — producing `overhangs[] = {type, quote, severity}`. Use:
an **uncertainty-tier** input and, at "severe/imminent", a **bear-floor deepening**.

Litigation reserves are tagged (`LossContingencyAccrualAtCarryingValue`,
`LossContingencyEstimateOfPossibleLoss`) and can be scaled by equity. ESG *scores*: the return
evidence is mixed-to-null once industry is controlled; controversy *events* are a tail-risk flag, not
a factor — do not build a score.

## 5. Liquidity, float & size

| Signal | Source | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Market-cap tier | ✅ `quote.marketCap` | < $2 B → uncertainty +1 tier |
| Average daily $ volume | Yahoo `averageDailyVolume3Month` × price | < $20 M → conviction demerit; position-size cap |
| Free float | Yahoo `floatShares` | < 50 % → uncertainty +1 |
| Bid-ask / days-to-exit | derived from ADV | sizing only |

The small-cap/illiquidity premium is real but compensates *risk*; it should raise the required E
for a BUY (11.md band widening), not lift the rating.

## 6. Event & tail risk

A typed `events[]` block (extracted by the grounded pass + a few XBRL rows):

| Event | Detect | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Debt maturity wall | `LongTermDebtMaturitiesRepaymentsOfPrincipalInNextTwelveMonths` (+ year-2) vs cash + FCF | if > cash + 2× FCF → distress tier up one |
| Warrant / convertible overhang | `ClassOfWarrantOrRightOutstanding`; text | potential dilution ≥ 10 % → conviction −, bear floor +0.05 (AMD ~20 % today, unrecorded) |
| Binary catalyst (FDA, court, contract) | text | uncertainty +1; STRONG labels suppressed |
| Pending acquisition / merger-arb | text, 8-K | rating **suspended → "under review"** (WBS precedent) |
| Earnings within 10 days | earnings calendar (Yahoo/FMP) | publish "pre-print" caveat |
| Index inclusion/exclusion | announcements | ignore for rating |

## 7. Probability calibration & horizon

- **Base rates.** With 400-day histories the book can produce its own table: P(≥15 % drawdown in
  12 m) and P(≥30 %) by realized-vol tercile. Literature and long-run data put a one-year ≥ 15 %
  drawdown near 40-50 % for a typical single stock and > 70 % for high-vol names — the desk's fixed
  25 % bear probability is almost certainly too low for semis and too high for utilities.
- **Implied-vol sanity check.** A one-σ 12-month move ≈ price × IV₁₂ₘ; if the analyst's bear
  price sits *inside* one σ, the bear is not a bear (deepen it or raise its probability).
- **Analyst overconfidence** is well documented (interval forecasts far too narrow); Morningstar's
  answer is an uncertainty rating that *widens the bands* rather than trusting point probabilities —
  adopt it (11.md).
- **Time decay.** A 12-month label built on a filing-date price silently ages. Rule: recompute E
  from the stored scenarios against the live price on each render; if the derived label changes, show
  "rating drift" and require a refresh at the next filing (see 11.md §5).
