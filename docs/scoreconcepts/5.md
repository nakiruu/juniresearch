# Concept 5 — The Cross-Sectional Multi-Factor Composite with Earnings-Quality & Distress Gates

## What this document is

A quant-style factor engine that produces one corroborating signal for the rating, built on three commitments that separate it from the scorecards in `1.md` and `2.md`:

1. **No absolute cutoffs.** Every raw metric is scored *relative* to a comparison set — the FactPack `peers`, the company's own five-year history, or a sector-median prior — never against a fixed number like "ROIC > 20% = 10 points." A rich P/E is only rich relative to something.
2. **Named, non-compensatory gates.** Earnings quality and financial distress are evaluated as validated academic constructs (Piotroski F-Score, Altman Z, a Sloan/Beneish accruals flag) that act as **vetoes and caps**. A great growth sleeve cannot buy back a failed gate.
3. **It corroborates, it does not replace.** The output plugs into the existing reward/risk spine in `lib/synth/conviction.ts` as a cap/veto/confirmation layer, not a second rating. `6.md` owns the final combination; this document supplies the factor-and-gate layer it consumes.

This document is deliberately scoped to the *cross-sectional* and *gate* layer. Moat and ROIC–WACC persistence live in `3.md`; intrinsic value / reverse-DCF lives in `4.md`; the meta-aggregation and uncertainty handling live in `6.md`.

---

## 0. Why the existing scorecards are shallow (and what changes)

`1.md` and `2.md` are flat, hand-weighted 0–100 sums over metrics scored against **absolute** thresholds:

- `2.md`: "ROIC > 20% → 10; 12–20% → 7; 6–12% → 4; < 6% → 0." "Net Debt/EBITDA < 1x → 10." "Forward P/E > 2.5× sector median → penalty."
- `1.md`: "Score = 20% Valuation + 20% Growth + 15% Quality + …", each pillar an average of absolutely-scored metrics.

Three structural problems follow, and this concept answers each:

| Problem in 1.md / 2.md | Consequence | Concept 5 answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute cutoffs** ("ROIC > 20% = 10") | Sector- and regime-blind. A 20% ROIC is elite for a utility and mediocre for a mature software name; a 15× EV/EBITDA is cheap in 2021 and dear in a high-rate regime. | Cross-sectional **z-score / percentile rank** vs peers + own history. The bar moves with the comparison set. |
| **Linear weighted sum** | Compensatory: a 96 growth score papers over an 8-point balance-sheet score, exactly the failure `2.md`'s veto list tries to patch after the fact. | **Rank aggregation** across sleeves + **hard gates evaluated first**. The gate is not a term in the sum; it is a switch on the output. |
| **Hand-set weights** (20/20/15/…) | Arbitrary and unstable; small weight changes reorder ratings. | Equal-risk / rank-based combination, so no single sleeve's scale dominates, and the weights are not free parameters to tune. |

`2.md` already gestures at gates (its Step-4 veto table and Altman Z mention). Concept 5 makes the gates the *spine of the non-compensatory logic* and specifies each construct's formula against the real FactPack fields, rather than listing thresholds prose-style.

## 1. Data surface and notation

Everything below is computed from a single FactPack (e.g. `data/facts/AMD/0000002488-26-000123.json`). Fields referenced by JSON path:

- **Panel** (5 fiscal years): `statements.fiscalYears`, `statements.income[*].values`, `statements.balance[*].values`, `statements.cashflow[*].values`. Row keys used: `revenue, grossProfit, operatingIncome, ebitda, netIncome, epsDiluted, cashAndInvestments, totalDebt, netDebt, totalEquity, currentRatio, operatingCashFlow, capex, buybacks, dividends, freeCashFlow`.
- **TTM ratios**: `ttm.{pe, ps, evToEbitda, grossMargin, operatingMargin, netMargin, netDebtToEbitda, interestCoverage, currentRatio, fcfYield}`.
- **Quote**: `quote.{price, marketCap, sharesOutstanding, week52Low, week52High, dividendYield}`.
- **Forward**: `estimates.nextFY.{revenue,eps}`, `estimates.followingFY.{revenue,eps}`.
- **Cross-section**: `peers[*].{ticker, pe, ps, evToEbitda}` (the intended comparison set).
- **Market**: `history[30].close`, `analysts.{consensusTarget, medianTarget, highTarget, lowTarget, buy, hold, sell}`.

Notation: let the subject company be `c`. Let `P = {c} ∪ peers` be the cross-section, `n = |P|`. Let `x_{c,t}` be metric `x` for the company in fiscal year `t ∈ {FY21…FY25}`. Sleeve scores are in *rank space* (higher = more attractive), so signs are flipped for "lower-is-better" metrics (all valuation multiples, leverage, realized volatility).

> **A load-bearing fact about the real data.** In every FactPack sampled (AMD, NVDA, V, JPM, LLY, AVGO, BAC), the `peers` block is populated with **tickers only** — `pe`, `ps`, `evToEbitda` are all `null` (provenance: `fmp company/peers (tickers only)`). The cross-sectional *valuation* comparison the charter asks for is therefore **not computable from today's pipeline**; it is a required data addition (§8). The engine below is designed to degrade gracefully to time-series and sector-median priors, and to flag low confidence, precisely because the peer numerics are usually absent. That degradation is a feature, not an apology.

## 2. Factor sleeves

Five sleeves, each a small basket of FactPack-derived metrics. The subset is chosen for what the data actually supports; where a classic factor input is missing, the sleeve is marked *low-confidence* rather than faked.

| Sleeve | FactPack inputs | Construction | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value** | `ttm.pe`, `ttm.ps`, `ttm.evToEbitda`, `ttm.fcfYield`, `quote.dividendYield` | Rank the earnings yield `1/pe`, sales yield `1/ps`, EBITDA yield `1/evToEbitda`, `fcfYield`, and shareholder yield (`fcfYield`-based buyback+dividend). Average the per-metric ranks. | Higher yield = cheaper = better |
| **Quality** | `ttm.grossMargin, operatingMargin, netMargin`; `ttm.interestCoverage`; panel `freeCashFlow/revenue`; `ttm.currentRatio`; derived ROIC (see `3.md`) | Rank margins, coverage, FCF margin, and capital returns. Quality overlaps `3.md` (moat/ROIC–WACC); here it is the *level and stability* of returns, not their durability. | Higher = better |
| **Growth** | `latestQuarter.revenueYoY`; panel revenue & EPS CAGR; `estimates.nextFY/followingFY` revenue & eps vs TTM | Rank trailing revenue CAGR, forward revenue growth `nextFY.revenue / TTM_rev − 1`, forward EPS growth, and growth *acceleration* (fwd minus trailing). | Higher = better |
| **Momentum** | `history[30].close`, `quote.{price, week52Low, week52High}` | 52-week range position `(price − low)/(high − low)`; short-window trend from the 30 closes; distance above/below the mean of the window. | Higher = better |
| **Low-Volatility** | `history[30].close` | Annualized realized vol of daily log returns over the window; optionally leverage-adjusted with `ttm.netDebtToEbitda`. | **Lower vol = better** (sign flipped) |

Design notes:

- **Quality vs `3.md`.** This sleeve captures the *cross-sectional level* of profitability. `3.md`'s moat engine captures *persistence* of ROIC–WACC spread. They are complementary inputs to `6.md`, not duplicates; if both are present, `6.md` should avoid double-counting by treating this sleeve as the point-estimate and `3.md` as the trend.
- **Momentum and Low-Vol are structurally weak here.** The FactPack ships only `history[30]` — ~29 daily returns. Classic 12–1 momentum and a stable realized-vol estimate need ≥252 closes. Both sleeves are therefore emitted with a **low-confidence flag** and down-weighted by `6.md` until a longer price history is added (§8). They are included for completeness and to make the missing-data path explicit, not because 30 closes carry a real low-vol signal.

## 3. Cross-sectional normalization (replacing absolute cutoffs)

### 3.1 Robust z-score, not mean/σ

For metric `x` over comparison set `P`, use **median and MAD** (median absolute deviation), not mean and standard deviation, so a single extreme peer cannot swamp the scale:

```
med   = median(x over P)
MAD   = median( |x_i − med| )
z_i   = (x_i − med) / (1.4826 · MAD)        # 1.4826 makes MAD ≈ σ for normal data
```

Then **winsorize** to tame outliers before ranking:

```
z_i  ← clip(z_i, −3, +3)
```

For "lower-is-better" metrics (every valuation multiple, leverage, realized vol), negate: `z_i ← −z_i`.

### 3.2 Percentile rank as the primary score

With small `n`, a percentile rank is more stable and interpretable than a z-score, and it is invariant to the metric's distribution shape:

```
rank_i = ( #{ j : x_j ≼ x_i } − 0.5 ) / n        # ≼ respects the metric's better-direction
```

The engine reports **both**: the robust z (for magnitude / gate math) and the percentile rank (for the composite). The composite in §5 is built on ranks.

### 3.3 Why rank/z beats fixed thresholds

- **Sector-aware for free.** If `P` is the sector peer set, "cheap" is defined by the sector's own multiples. A 20× EV/EBITDA semiconductor name and a 9× EV/EBITDA bank are each scored against their own kind. `2.md`'s "Net Debt/EBITDA < 1x = 10" penalizes an investment bank for a leverage profile that is normal for its industry; a rank does not.
- **Regime-aware for free.** As the whole peer set re-rates in a new rate regime, the *relative* ordering is preserved even as absolute multiples drift. A fixed "P/E < 15 = cheap" ages badly; a percentile does not.
- **Monotone, not cliff-edged.** Absolute buckets create discontinuities (ROIC 19.9% scores 7, 20.1% scores 10). Ranks and clipped z are smooth.

### 3.4 The small-peer problem (n ≈ 4) and the fallback ladder

The FactPack peer set is ~4 names, and (today) their multiples are `null`. A four-point cross-section is statistically thin — the MAD is unstable and a single peer swings every rank. The engine handles this with an explicit **confidence ladder** and **shrinkage**, in this order:

1. **Peer cross-section** (preferred): use `P` if ≥ 3 peers carry a numeric value for the metric. Confidence scales with `n_valid`.
2. **Time-series z** (always available): score the metric against the company's **own 5-year history** from `statements`. This is fully computable today and is the backbone of the AMD worked example (§7). It answers "is this figure high or low *for this company*," which is regime-aware in a different way (it normalizes out the company's structural level).
3. **Sector-median prior**: a small static table of sector-median multiples/margins (`data/desk/sector-priors.json`, to be added) used as a one-point anchor when peers are empty. Score as `z = (x_c − prior_med) / prior_scale`.
4. **Shrinkage toward the prior.** When `n_valid` is small, shrink the peer statistic toward the sector prior with weight `w = n_valid / (n_valid + k)` (Empirical-Bayes; `k ≈ 3`):

```
med*   = w · med_peers + (1 − w) · prior_med
scale* = w · MAD_peers + (1 − w) · prior_scale
```

5. **Confidence flag** attached to every sleeve score: `high` (peer cross-section, n_valid ≥ 3), `medium` (time-series or shrunk), `low` (single-source or window-limited, e.g. Momentum/Low-Vol on 30 closes). `6.md` widens the uncertainty band and pulls the label toward HOLD when sleeve confidence is `low`.

The key discipline: **a missing peer number never silently becomes a zero or a neutral 50th percentile.** It routes to the next rung and lowers confidence.

## 4. Earnings-quality & distress GATES (named constructs, non-compensatory)

Gates are computed **before** the composite and can only move the rating **toward caution**. Each is a validated construct; each is specified against the real fields, including the honest note of which inputs the FactPack does not yet expose.

### 4.1 Piotroski F-Score (fundamental quality, 0–9)

Nine binary signals from the panel; the year compared is the latest fiscal transition (FY24→FY25 in the AMD pack). Because the FactPack does **not** carry total assets, ROA and asset-turnover components use a documented **invested-capital proxy** `IC = totalEquity + totalDebt` and are flagged; when true total assets are added (§8) they replace the proxy verbatim.

| # | Signal | Formula against FactPack | Point if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ROA > 0 | `netIncome_FY25 > 0` (proxy ROA `NI/IC`) | positive |
| 2 | Operating cash flow > 0 | `operatingCashFlow_FY25 > 0` | positive |
| 3 | ΔROA > 0 | `NI/IC` rising FY24→FY25 | improving |
| 4 | Accruals: CFO > NI | `operatingCashFlow_FY25 > netIncome_FY25` | cash-backed |
| 5 | ΔLeverage < 0 | `totalDebt/IC` **falling** FY24→FY25 | de-levering |
| 6 | ΔCurrent ratio > 0 | `balance.currentRatio` rising FY24→FY25 | improving |
| 7 | No new shares | diluted shares (`NI/epsDiluted`) not rising, or `buybacks < 0` | no dilution |
| 8 | ΔGross margin > 0 | `grossProfit/revenue` rising FY24→FY25 | improving |
| 9 | ΔAsset turnover > 0 | `revenue/IC` rising FY24→FY25 | improving |

**Gate action.** F-Score is a **cap**, not an additive term:
- `F ≤ 2` → cap the rating at **HOLD** (a low-quality name cannot be a BUY on factors alone).
- `F ≤ 1` with negative FCF trend → cap at **SELL**.
- `F ≥ 7` → **no cap** (removes a brake; it never *adds* upside — upside must come from E/R and the composite).

### 4.2 Altman Z-Score (distress / bankruptcy risk)

Classic Z (manufacturers) and Z″ (non-manufacturers/EM) both need balance-sheet detail the FactPack lacks:

```
Z   = 1.2·X1 + 1.4·X2 + 3.3·X3 + 0.6·X4 + 1.0·X5
Z'' = 6.56·X1 + 3.26·X2 + 6.72·X3 + 1.05·X4
X1 = Working Capital / Total Assets
X2 = Retained Earnings / Total Assets
X3 = EBIT (operatingIncome) / Total Assets
X4 = Market Value of Equity (quote.marketCap) / Total Liabilities
X5 = Sales (revenue) / Total Assets
```

`operatingIncome`, `marketCap`, and `revenue` are present, but **Total Assets, Total Liabilities, Working Capital (dollar), and Retained Earnings are not** in the current FactPack. So the *full* Altman Z **cannot be computed today** — it is a required data addition (§8).

Until those fields exist, the engine runs an **interim distress proxy** built entirely from available ratios, calibrated to the same three-zone logic:

```
distressProxy passes (SAFE) when ALL of:
  ttm.interestCoverage  ≥ 3          # EBIT covers interest ≥ 3×
  ttm.netDebtToEbitda   ≤ 3          # or < 0 (net cash)
  ttm.currentRatio      ≥ 1.0
  freeCashFlow_FY25     > 0  OR  cashAndInvestments covers > 2y of any FCF burn

distressProxy DISTRESS when interestCoverage < 1  OR  netDebtToEbitda > 5
                                 OR (currentRatio < 1 AND FCF < 0 AND cash < 1y burn)
GREY zone otherwise.
```

**Gate action** (non-compensatory, overrides everything including E/R):
- **DISTRESS** → cap at **SELL**; if the MD&A/risk text carries going-concern or covenant-breach language, cap at **STRONG SELL**.
- **GREY** → cap at **HOLD** and force the `low` confidence flag.
- **SAFE** → no cap.

This mirrors `2.md`'s "FCF negative AND Interest Coverage < 3 → cap" intent, but as a named zone model rather than an ad-hoc pair of thresholds, and it is the *only* rung that can push below SELL.

### 4.3 Earnings-manipulation / low-accruals flag (Sloan; Beneish where feasible)

**Sloan accruals** — the workhorse, computable today with a proxy denominator:

```
Accrual ratio (cash-flow form) = (netIncome − operatingCashFlow) / avg(IC)
Cash conversion                = operatingCashFlow / netIncome
```

High positive accruals (earnings far exceeding cash flow) is the classic Sloan low-quality signal that predicts future underperformance. Rule:

```
accrualFlag = HIGH  if  accrualRatio > +10%   (earnings not cash-backed)
            = LOW   if  accrualRatio < 0       (conservative, cash-backed)
```

**Beneish M-Score** — the eight-variable manipulation model (DSRI, GMI, AQI, SGI, DEPI, SGAI, LVGI, TATA) needs **receivables, total assets, PP&E, depreciation, and SG&A**, none of which the FactPack exposes. Beneish is therefore **not computable today** and is listed under required data (§8). Of its eight terms, only SGI (`revenue` growth) and a gross-margin GMI proxy (`grossProfit/revenue`) can be formed from current fields — insufficient for a valid M-Score, so the engine does **not** emit a partial M and mislabel it.

**Gate action:**
- `accrualFlag = HIGH` (and F-Score not already strong) → cap at **HOLD** and flag `earnings-quality`. Rationale: expensive names whose earnings are not cash-backed are exactly where a factor sum most over-rewards; the cap is the point.
- `accrualFlag = LOW` → no cap (a quality *tailwind* is expressed through the Quality sleeve, not the gate).

### 4.4 Gate summary — the caps table

| Gate | Condition | Cap on final label |
|---|---|---|
| Distress | interim proxy = DISTRESS (or Altman Z < 1.8 once available) | **SELL** (→ STRONG SELL on going-concern) |
| Distress | GREY zone | **HOLD** + low confidence |
| Piotroski | F ≤ 2 | **HOLD** |
| Piotroski | F ≤ 1 and FCF trend negative | **SELL** |
| Accruals | accrualRatio > +10% | **HOLD** + earnings-quality flag |

Caps are applied as `min(label, cap)` on the SELL→STRONG BUY ordering. Multiple caps take the most conservative. **No gate can ever raise a label** — that is what "non-compensatory" means here.

## 5. Combining the sleeves (rank aggregation, gate-first)

### 5.1 Composite = mean of sleeve percentile ranks

Within each sleeve, average the per-metric percentile ranks into a sleeve rank `R_s ∈ [0,1]`. The composite is the **mean of sleeve ranks**, optionally confidence-weighted:

```
Composite = Σ_s ( conf_s · R_s ) / Σ_s conf_s        # conf_s ∈ {1.0 high, 0.6 medium, 0.3 low}
CompositePct = 100 · Composite
```

### 5.2 Why rank aggregation beats hand-set percentage weights

- **Scale-free.** Ranks are all in [0,1], so no sleeve dominates because its raw units are larger. `1.md`'s "20% Valuation + 20% Growth + …" implicitly assumes the 0–100 sub-scores are comparably calibrated; they are not.
- **Weights stop being free parameters.** Equal-confidence rank averaging removes the 20/20/15/15/… guesswork that makes `1.md`/`2.md` fragile. Confidence weighting is *data-driven* (how many valid peers, how long the history), not analyst taste.
- **Robust to one bad sleeve.** A single sleeve at the 5th percentile pulls the mean modestly; it cannot be "bought back" to the top by another sleeve because the *gates*, not the sum, hold the veto.

An **equal-risk** variant (weight each sleeve inversely to the cross-sectional dispersion of its rank) is available where a sleeve is near-degenerate (all peers similar); it prevents a noise-dominated sleeve from carrying weight. Equal-confidence is the default for `n ≈ 4`.

### 5.3 One strong sleeve cannot mask a failed gate

Order of operations is the whole point:

```
1. gates   = evaluateGates(factpack)          # distress, Piotroski, accruals  → caps
2. sleeves = crossSectionalSleeves(factpack)  # 5 ranks + confidence
3. composite, compositePct, confidence
4. label   = reconcile(E_R_label, gates, composite, confidence)   # §6
```

The composite is computed *after* the gates and can only act **within** the ceiling the gates set. A 95th-percentile Growth sleeve on a name that trips the distress gate still yields a SELL-capped label.

## 6. Mapping to the 5 buckets and reconciling with conviction (E, R)

The rating spine stays `lib/synth/conviction.ts`: expected upside `E`, reward/risk `R = E/D`, mapped to a label by `deriveLabel` against `data/desk/desk.json` thresholds. Concept 5 is a **corroborator and cap**, never a parallel rating. This respects the existing rule that the author may sit *one notch more conservative* than the E/R label (`conservativeNotch`) but never more aggressive.

### 6.1 The composite's own bucket (for transparency)

The composite maps to a 5-bucket view by **cross-sectional percentile**, not absolute score — the mapping itself obeys the no-absolute-cutoff rule:

| CompositePct (within peer + history context) | Composite view |
|---|---|
| ≥ 80 | STRONG BUY-like |
| 60–80 | BUY-like |
| 40–60 | HOLD-like |
| 20–40 | SELL-like |
| < 20 | STRONG SELL-like |

This view is **diagnostic only**. It never becomes the rating on its own; it feeds the reconciliation.

### 6.2 Reconciliation with E/R — the wrap around `conviction.ts`

```ts
// pseudo-code; the real combiner lives with 6.md's aggregation layer
const c        = computeConviction(scenarios, price);      // existing
const labelER  = deriveLabel(c, desk.rating);              // existing E/R label
const gates    = evaluateGates(factpack);                  // §4
const comp     = crossSectionalComposite(factpack);        // §5  -> {pct, view, confidence}

function reconcile(labelER, gates, comp) {
  // 1. GATES: hard caps, most conservative wins, applied on SELL→STRONG BUY order
  let label = applyCaps(labelER, gates.caps);              // distress / Piotroski / accruals

  // 2. CORROBORATION: a bullish E/R must be confirmed by the composite
  if (isBullish(labelER) && comp.view is HOLD-like-or-worse) {
    label = conservativeNotch(label) ?? label;             // step one notch toward HOLD
  }
  if (isBullish(labelER) && valueSleeveBottomDecile(comp)) {
    label = conservativeNotch(label) ?? label;             // "great business, wrong price"
  }

  // 3. LOW CONFIDENCE (thin peers, 30-day history) never emboldens
  if (comp.confidence === "low" && isBullish(label)) {
    label = conservativeNotch(label) ?? label;
  }

  // 4. CONTRARIAN LIFT is bounded: a bombed-out SELL may lift ONE notch toward HOLD
  //    only if all gates pass, composite top-quartile, and Value sleeve cheap.
  if (labelER === "SELL" && gates.allPass && comp.pct >= 75 && valueCheap(comp)) {
    label = "HOLD";                                        // never above HOLD from a SELL
  }
  return label;
}
```

The concrete rules the charter asks for, stated plainly:

- **A BUY on E/R that fails the earnings-quality gate (F ≤ 2 or accrualRatio > 10%) is capped at HOLD.**
- **A distress flag caps at SELL** (going-concern → STRONG SELL), overriding any E/R upside.
- **A BUY on E/R whose composite is only HOLD-like, or whose Value sleeve is bottom-decile, takes the one-notch-conservative step** (BUY→HOLD) — it does not stay BUY on reward/risk alone.
- **The composite can never make the label more aggressive than the E/R label** — it can only confirm, or trigger the existing conservative notch, or (bounded) lift a SELL toward HOLD when the gates all pass and the name is genuinely cheap.

This keeps `conviction.ts` as the load-bearing element and adds the independent fundamental layer the project was missing, without letting a factor sum overrule the scenario math or vice-versa. `6.md` owns the final assembly (uncertainty bands, how `3.md`/`4.md` also fold in); Concept 5 hands it `{caps, compositePct, sleeveRanks, confidence}`.

## 7. Worked example — AMD (`0000002488-26-000123.json`)

Price $504.20 (2026-09-15); FY21–FY25 panel; peers `[AMAT, ARM, ASML, KLAC]` with **all multiples null**.

### 7.1 Cross-sectional valuation — the fallback fires

AMD's `ttm`: `pe = 127.97`, `ps = 19.90`, `evToEbitda = 76.63`, `fcfYield = 1.02%`. The four peers carry `null` for every multiple, so **rung 1 (peer cross-section) is unavailable** — exactly the systemic gap in §1. The engine drops to rung 3 (sector-median prior) with a `low`→`medium` confidence flag. Against any plausible semiconductor sector prior (median EV/EBITDA in the high-teens to twenties), a 76.6× EV/EBITDA and 20× sales sit in the **bottom decile on Value** — AMD is expensive on every multiple. `fcfYield` of 1.0% corroborates. **Value sleeve rank ≈ 0.05–0.15, confidence medium.**

This is the honest headline of the example: *the peer-relative valuation the charter wants is blocked by null peer data*, and the design's job is to fail loudly and route to the prior, not to invent a rank.

### 7.2 Time-series z-scores (fully computable, rung 2)

Where the cross-section is thin, the own-history normalization does real work. Robust/normal z of the FY25 value against the FY21–FY25 series:

**Gross margin** (`grossProfit/revenue`): FY21–25 = 48.25%, 44.93%, 46.12%, 49.35%, **49.52%**. Mean 47.63%, σ 1.82%.
```
z(FY25 gross margin) = (49.52 − 47.63) / 1.82 = +1.04
```
**Operating margin** (`operatingIncome/revenue`): 22.20%, 5.36%, 1.77%, 7.37%, **10.66%**. Mean 9.47%, σ 6.99%.
```
z(FY25 operating margin) = (10.66 − 9.47) / 6.99 = +0.17
```
**FCF margin** (`freeCashFlow/revenue`): 19.59%, 13.20%, 4.94%, 9.33%, **19.44%**. Mean 13.30%, σ 5.71%.
```
z(FY25 FCF margin) = (19.44 − 13.30) / 5.71 = +1.08
```

Reading: gross margin and FCF margin are ~1σ above AMD's own five-year norm (genuine quality improvement as the Data-Center mix grew); operating margin is only in line with its own history because FY21 was an unusually high base and opex is scaling with the AI ramp. A fixed "operating margin > 25% = 10" cutoff (`2.md`) would have scored AMD's 10.7% as a flat 4/10; the time-series z correctly says "in line for this company, improving off the trough." **Quality sleeve rank ≈ 0.70 (time-series), confidence medium.**

### 7.3 Growth and Momentum

- **Growth:** `latestQuarter.revenueYoY = +50.1%`; trailing FY25 revenue growth `34,639/25,785 − 1 = +34.3%`; forward `estimates.nextFY.revenue 50,873.7 / TTM ≈ +47%`; forward EPS `7.619 / 2.65 − 1 = +187%`. Every metric is top-of-cross-section. **Growth sleeve rank ≈ 0.95, confidence high** (forward estimates are populated).
- **Momentum:** 52-week range position `(504.20 − 149.85)/(584.73 − 149.85) = 81.5%` (near the high). The 30 closes run 484.64 → 493.41, ≈ +1.8% over the window — flat-to-up. **Momentum rank ≈ 0.55, confidence low** (window too short for 12–1).
- **Low-Vol:** annualized realized vol of the 29 daily returns ≈ **3.4%/day → ~55% annualized** — high. **Low-Vol rank ≈ 0.15, confidence low.** Flagged and down-weighted; 30 closes are not a real low-vol estimate.

### 7.4 Gates

**Piotroski F-Score (FY24→FY25):**

| # | Signal | Numbers | Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ROA > 0 | NI FY25 = $4,335M > 0 | 1 |
| 2 | OCF > 0 | OCF FY25 = $7,709M > 0 | 1 |
| 3 | ΔROA > 0 | NI/IC: 6.4% (4,335/67,471) vs 2.7% (1,641/59,780) | 1 |
| 4 | CFO > NI | 7,709 > 4,335 | 1 |
| 5 | ΔLeverage < 0 | totalDebt 4,472 vs 2,212 → debt/IC **rose** 3.7%→6.6% | **0** |

Confidence-weighted mean of sleeve ranks (Value 0.10 med, Quality 0.70 med, Growth 0.95 high, Momentum 0.55 low, Low-Vol 0.15 low):

```

A high-quality, high-growth business dragged into the HOLD-like band by valuation — a "great company, rich price" profile. Now the E/R spine, using analyst targets as a scenario proxy (the real scenarios come from `/synthesize`):

```

## 8. Limitations and required new data

**Blocking data gaps (the engine flags, does not fake):**

| Needed field | Enables | Status in FactPack |
|---|---|---|
| `peers[*].pe / ps / evToEbitda` **populated** | The peer-relative *valuation* cross-section — the core charter ask | **Always null today** (tickers-only from FMP). Highest-value addition. |
| Total assets, total liabilities | Altman Z / Z″ (full distress), true Piotroski ROA & asset turnover | Absent. Currently proxied by `IC = equity + debt`, flagged. |
| Working capital ($), retained earnings | Altman X1, X2 | Absent. |
| Receivables, PP&E, depreciation, SG&A | Beneish M-Score (DSRI, AQI, DEPI, SGAI, TATA) | Absent. Beneish **not computed**; only SGI/GMI proxies form, insufficient alone. |
| ≥ 252 daily closes | Real 12–1 Momentum and stable realized-vol Low-Vol sleeves | Only `history[30]`. Both sleeves emit `low` confidence. |
| Estimate *revision* history (30/90-day) | A revisions sleeve (as `1.md` §6 wants) | Only point-in-time `estimates`; no revision series. |
| Sector-median prior table (`data/desk/sector-priors.json`) | Fallback rung 3 when peers are null | Does not exist yet; must be built. |

**Methodological limits:**

- **Thin cross-section.** `n ≈ 4` makes MAD and ranks noisy; the shrinkage + confidence ladder (§3.4) mitigates but cannot manufacture signal. Any single-peer swing should be inspected.
- **Point-in-time / survivorship.** Peer lists and sector priors must be as-of the filing date to avoid look-ahead; the FactPack's `capturedAt` should govern.
- **Proxy transparency.** Every proxy (IC-for-assets, interim distress zones, target-based E/R scenarios) is labelled in output so a reviewer never mistakes a proxy for the named construct. When the real fields arrive, the formulas above are drop-in replacements — no re-architecture.
- **Sector edge cases.** Banks and insurers break several sleeves (gross margin, EV/EBITDA, current ratio are meaningless), as the desk's bank runs already document. Concept 5 should carry a `sector` switch that disables inapplicable metrics and leans on the time-series and ROE/ROTCE-style inputs — consistent with `1.md` §15 and the moat logic in `3.md`.

## 9. Why this is deeper than `1.md` / `2.md` — in one table

| Dimension | `1.md` / `2.md` | Concept 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Metric scoring | **Absolute** cutoffs (ROIC > 20% = 10) | **Cross-sectional** robust z / percentile vs peers + own history + sector prior |
| Sector/regime awareness | None (fixed numbers) | Built in (the bar is the comparison set) |
| Combination | Hand-set % weights, linear sum | Rank aggregation, confidence-weighted, scale-free |
| Compensation | Compensatory (+ bolt-on veto list) | **Gate-first, non-compensatory** by construction |
| Quality/distress | Prose thresholds, a Z mention | **Named constructs** (Piotroski 9-component, Altman zones, Sloan/Beneish) with formulas mapped to real fields |
| Missing data | Silently scores or ignores | Explicit fallback ladder + confidence flag; refuses to fake a named construct |
| Relationship to the rating | A parallel 0–100 that competes with E/R | A **cap/veto/corroborator** wrapping `conviction.ts`, feeding `6.md` |

The one-line statement of the concept: *score every factor relative to something, gate the rating on named quality and solvency constructs that a sum cannot override, and let the result confirm or restrain the reward/risk label rather than replace it.*
