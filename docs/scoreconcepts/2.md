# Algorithmic Stock Rating Framework
### One-Year Horizon | Strong Sell / Sell / Hold / Buy / Strong Buy

---

## Purpose

This framework converts financial statements, valuation multiples, growth metrics, and risk/reward into a repeatable rating.

It is designed to prevent a common mistake: letting a wide bull/bear spread or a large expected upside override weak cash flow, high leverage, or ongoing dilution.

**Core principle:**

> Risk/reward is a modifier, not the main driver.  
> Fundamentals and cash flow do most of the work.

---

## 1. Core Questions

A one-year rating should answer three questions in order:

1. **Is the business financially healthy enough to survive the next 12 months?**
   - Free cash flow
   - Balance sheet
   - Interest coverage
   - Dilution

2. **Is it growing and profitable enough to justify owning it?**
   - Revenue growth
   - ROIC
   - Margins
   - Backlog / RPO

3. **Is the price attractive relative to that reality?**
   - Valuation
   - Expected value
   - Risk/reward

A stock can have +21% expected upside and a -36.7% bear case and still be a **Hold** if free cash flow is negative and the balance sheet is stretched.

## 2. The Algorithm

### Step 1 — Compute Raw Metrics

Use TTM and forward estimates where available.

| Metric | Formula | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| FCF | `OCF - CapEx` | Real cash generated after investment |
| FCF Margin | `FCF / Revenue` | How much cash the business keeps |
| FCF Yield | `FCF / Market Cap` | Cash return to owners |
| ROIC | `EBIT × (1 - Tax Rate) / (Total Debt + Equity - Cash)` | Return on all capital |
| ROIC-WACC Spread | `ROIC - WACC` | Value creation above cost of capital |
| Revenue Growth | `(Rev_t / Rev_{t-1}) - 1` | Top-line momentum |
| Forward Revenue Growth | `Consensus next FY Rev / TTM Rev - 1` | Near-term visibility |
| EPS Growth | `Diluted EPS_t / EPS_{t-1} - 1` | Earnings momentum |
| Gross Margin | `Gross Profit / Revenue` | Pricing power / mix |
| Operating Margin | `Operating Income / Revenue` | Core profitability |
| Net Debt / EBITDA | `(Total Debt - Cash) / EBITDA` | Leverage |
| Interest Coverage | `EBIT / Interest Expense` | Ability to service debt |
| Current Ratio | `Current Assets / Current Liabilities` | Short-term liquidity |
| Share Count Change | `Shares_t / Shares_{t-1} - 1` | Dilution / buybacks |
| CapEx / OCF | `CapEx / Operating Cash Flow` | Capital intensity |
| RPO Coverage | `RPO / TTM Revenue` | Backlog visibility |
| Expected Value | `Σ (Probability × Return)` | Probability-weighted upside |
| Reward/Risk | `Expected Upside / |Expected Downside|` | Asymmetry |

---

### Step 2 — Score Each Metric from 0 to 10

#### FCF & Cash Generation

| Metric | 10 | 7 | 4 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FCF Margin | >25% | 10–25% | 0–10% | Negative |
| FCF Yield | >8% | 4–8% | 0–4% | Negative |
| CapEx / OCF | <40% | 40–70% | 70–100% | >100% |
| FCF Trend | Rising | Stable | Falling | Negative and worsening |

#### Profitability / ROIC

| Metric | 10 | 7 | 4 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROIC | >20% | 12–20% | 6–12% | <6% |
| ROIC-WACC | >10% | 4–10% | 0–4% | Negative |
| Operating Margin | >25% | 15–25% | 8–15% | <8% |
| Gross Margin Trend | Rising | Stable | Slight fall | Sharp fall |

#### Growth

| Metric | 10 | 7 | 4 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue Growth | >20% | 10–20% | 3–10% | <3% or negative |
| Forward Revenue Growth | >20% | 10–20% | 3–10% | <3% |
| EPS Growth | >20% | 10–20% | 3–10% | <3% |
| RPO / Revenue | >3x | 1.5–3x | 0.8–1.5x | <0.8x |

#### Balance Sheet

| Metric | 10 | 7 | 4 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net Debt / EBITDA | <1x | 1–2x | 2–3.5x | >3.5x |
| Interest Coverage | >10x | 5–10x | 3–5x | <3x |
| Current Ratio | >1.5 | 1.2–1.5 | 1.0–1.2 | <1.0 |
| Share Count Change | Buybacks | Flat | 0–3% dilution | >3% dilution |

#### Valuation

| Metric | 10 | 7 | 4 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forward P/E vs 5Y avg | >20% below | 0–20% below | In line | Above |
| EV/EBITDA vs 5Y avg | >20% below | 0–20% below | In line | Above |
| PEG | <1.0 | 1.0–1.5 | 1.5–2.0 | >2.0 |
| FCF Yield | >8% | 4–8% | 0–4% | Negative |

#### Momentum / Catalysts

| Metric | 10 | 7 | 4 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price vs 200-day MA | >10% above | 0–10% above | 0–10% below | >10% below |
| Earnings Surprise | Beat and raise | Beat | In line | Miss |
| Analyst Revisions | Rising | Stable | Falling | Sharp cuts |
| Catalyst Visibility | Clear near-term | Some | Vague | None |

#### Risk/Reward

Use **probability-weighted expected value**, not simple upside/downside.

\[
EV = (P_{bull} \times R_{bull}) + (P_{base} \times R_{base}) + (P_{bear} \times R_{bear})
\]

Where \(R\) = return from current price to target.

| Condition | Score |
|---|---|
| EV > 20% and Reward/Risk > 2.0 | 10 |
| EV 10–20% and Reward/Risk 1.5–2.0 | 8 |
| EV 0–10% and Reward/Risk 1.0–1.5 | 6 |
| EV < 0 | 0 |

---

### Step 3 — Weight the Pillars

For a **one-year horizon**, use these weights:

| Pillar | Weight |
|---|---|
| FCF & Cash Generation | 20% |
| Valuation | 20% |
| Profitability / ROIC | 15% |
| Growth | 15% |
| Balance Sheet / Financial Health | 15% |
| Momentum / Catalysts | 10% |
| Risk/Reward | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Composite score:

\[
S = 0.20F + 0.20V + 0.15P + 0.15G + 0.15B + 0.10M + 0.05R
\]

Where each pillar is the average of its metric scores, scaled 0–10.  
Then multiply by 10 to get a 0–100 score.

---

### Step 4 — Apply Hard Vetoes / Caps

These prevent a high growth score from masking fatal financial risk.

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| FCF negative AND Net Debt/EBITDA > 3.0 | Cap composite at **55 (Hold)** |
| FCF negative AND Interest Coverage < 3.0 | Cap composite at **45 (Sell/Hold)** |
| Share count growth > 5% | Subtract **5 points** |
| ROIC < WACC | Subtract **5 points** |
| FCF negative AND CapEx/OCF > 100% | Subtract **5 points** |
| RPO/Revenue > 3x AND FCF negative | Add **3 points** (visibility credit) |
| Going-concern language or covenant breach | Immediate **Strong Sell** |

---

### Step 5 — Map Composite Score to Rating

| Composite Score | Rating |
|---|---|
| 80–100 | **Strong Buy** |
| 65–79 | **Buy** |
| 45–64 | **Hold** |
| 30–44 | **Sell** |
| 0–29 | **Strong Sell** |

## 3. Example: ORCL

Using the two ORCL PDFs provided:

| Pillar | Score (0–10) | Weight | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| FCF & Cash Generation | 1 | 20% | 0.20 |
| Valuation | 8 | 20% | 1.60 |
| Profitability / ROIC | 5 | 15% | 0.75 |
| Growth | 9 | 15% | 1.35 |
| Balance Sheet | 4 | 15% | 0.60 |
| Momentum / Catalysts | 6 | 10% | 0.60 |
| Risk/Reward | 6 | 5% | 0.30 |
| **Composite** |  |  | **5.40 → 54/100** |

Then apply the hard veto:

> **FCF negative + Net Debt/EBITDA ~3.2x → cap at 55 (Hold).**

So this framework would rate ORCL **Hold**, even though the newer PDF’s risk/reward math gives +21% expected upside and 0.57× reward/risk.

The reason:

- Negative free cash flow
- High leverage
- Dilution
- Capex still rising

Risk/reward is not ignored — it just cannot override the cash-flow reality.

---

## 4. Why This Is Better Than Pure Risk/Reward

A pure risk/reward ratio can be manipulated by:

- Optimistic bull targets
- A wide bear target that never gets probability-weighted
- Ignoring whether the company can fund itself
- Ignoring dilution
- Ignoring FCF inflection timing

This framework forces the rating to be anchored in:

- **Cash flow** — can the company self-fund?
- **ROIC** — does growth create value?
- **Balance sheet** — can it survive a credit shock?
- **Growth quality** — is revenue real and contracted?
- **Valuation** — what are you paying for it?
- **Risk/reward** — only as a final modifier

---

## 5. One-Line Rule

**Buy** when the business generates cash, earns above its cost of capital, grows, and trades at a reasonable price with positive expected value.

**Hold** when the story is good but the cash flow or balance sheet has not yet confirmed it.

**Sell** when cash flow is negative, leverage is high, dilution is rising, and the valuation already prices in a recovery that has not arrived.
