# 9 — Missing factors II: expectations dynamics, revisions, sentiment, positioning & momentum

Research date 2026-09-22. The built system's only "expectations" read is the reverse DCF (what the
price implies vs the company's own history). It never asks what the *Street* is doing — revisions,
surprises, dispersion, positioning — even though two of those are already in every FactPack.

## Executive summary

1. **Estimate revisions are the best-documented short-horizon return predictor the system ignores.**
   Zacks Rank is built on nothing else (Agreement, Magnitude, Upside, Surprise) and StarMine's
   Analyst Revisions Model is the top-weighted input to its Combined Alpha Model.
2. The pipeline captures `analysts` and `estimates` **point-in-time only**. Revisions need a
   *history*; the fix is to snapshot those two blocks on every capture (and on a schedule) — a data
   design change, not a new source.
3. **Two revision-adjacent signals are computable today and unbuilt:** analyst-target dispersion
   (6.md §4.1; AMD's is 1.58 → maximal) and the Street-vs-model divergence flag (4.md §8).
4. **Price momentum is nearly absent** (52-week-range position only, 30 days of history). 12-1
   momentum, the 52-week-high effect and one-month reversal are among the most robust anomalies;
   they need 252+ closes from the Yahoo chart endpoint the free source already calls.
5. **Post-earnings-announcement drift** (surprise → multi-month drift) needs actual-vs-consensus EPS
   at each print; FMP and Yahoo both expose a surprise history keylessly.
6. **Positioning** (short interest, 13F changes, insider buys) has real but smaller evidence; short
   interest is the strongest and is one Yahoo field (`shortPercentOfFloat`).
7. **Text tone** from the transcript/headlines already in the pack is a modest, LLM-scorable factor
   with grounding rules the desk already enforces — a confidence input, not a rating driver.
8. **Crowdedness** (near-unanimous Buys with price at target) is the mirror of MoS and should widen
   the uncertainty tier (11.md) rather than move the label.

## 1. Estimate revisions & earnings momentum

**Practice.** Zacks Rank = Agreement (share of analysts revising up), Magnitude (60-day change in
consensus for this and next FY), Upside (most-accurate vs consensus) and Surprise (recent EPS
surprises), recomputed nightly ([Zacks Rank guide](https://advisortools.zacks.com/Content/ZAT_ZacksRankGuide.pdf),
[Zacks education](https://www.zacks.com/education/stock-education/zacks-rank-guide-9)). StarMine's
ARM ranks on revisions to EPS, revenue and EBITDA plus recommendation changes, with SmartEstimates
weighting more accurate analysts, and ARM is one of eight inputs to the Combined Alpha Model
([LSEG ARM](https://www.lseg.com/en/data-catalogue/analytics/quantitative-analytics/starmine-analyst-revisions-model),
[LSEG CAM](https://www.lseg.com/en/data-catalogue/analytics/quantitative-analytics/starmine-combined-alpha-model)).

**Evidence.** Revision and earnings-momentum strategies (Givoly–Lakonishok; Chan–Jegadeesh–Lakonishok
1996) earn abnormal returns over 6-12 months; PEAD — prices drifting in the direction of the
surprise for weeks to months — is among the most persistent anomalies, moderated by analyst
responsiveness and forecast dispersion ([Caltech PEAD survey](https://jkatz.caltech.edu/documents/28622/peads.pdf),
[JAE on analyst responsiveness](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410108000220),
[dispersion × earnings momentum](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227497080_Dispersion_in_Analyst_Forecasts_and_the_Profitability_of_Earnings_Momentum_Strategies)).

**Formulas.**
```
rev30  = consensusEPS_nextFY(t) / consensusEPS_nextFY(t−30d) − 1      (magnitude)
rev90  = same over 90 days
breadth = (#up − #down) / #analysts                                      (agreement; needs per-analyst data or a proxy from count deltas)
surpriseZ = (actualEPS − consensusEPS) / σ(consensus)  at each print; drift = mean of last 4
```
**Data design.** Add `data/snapshots/<TICKER>/<YYYY-MM-DD>.json` = `{analysts, estimates, quote.price}`
written by `facts:enrich` and by a lightweight scheduled `facts:snapshot` (Yahoo `earningsTrend` +
`financialData` are already parsed in `lib/facts/free/yahoo.ts`). Revisions become a diff over
snapshots; nothing else in the pipeline changes. Point-in-time correctness is automatic because each
snapshot is dated at capture.

**Placement.** Revision momentum is a **composite sleeve** ("Expectations") with a 3-6-month decay;
a sharp negative revision cluster is also a **conviction demerit** on any bullish label.

## 2. Street-vs-model divergence and dispersion (buildable now)

- **Dispersion** `(highTarget − lowTarget)/medianTarget`: AMD (260/625/1250) = **1.58**; NVDA is
  far tighter. 6.md §4.1 names it the first conviction input; `decide.ts` does not use it (audit I13).
- **Recommendation skew** `buy/(buy+hold+sell)`: near 1.0 with price ≥ consensus target is the
  crowded state (§6).
- **Divergence** `|E_mech − E_Street|` where `E_Street = consensusTarget/price − 1`: 4.md's flag at
  0.25 with a one-way cap (audit I12). Target-price literature (Brav–Lehavy 2003; Bradshaw et al.)
  finds consensus targets carry information but are systematically optimistic (~15-20 % implied
  upside on average) — so the *level* of Street E should be haircut, while *changes* in it are the
  signal.

**Placement.** Dispersion → **uncertainty tier / conviction**; divergence → **conviction + a flag in
the prose**; Street E level → never a rating driver.

## 3. Price momentum done properly

**Evidence.** 12-1 momentum (Jegadeesh–Titman 1993; Fama–French UMD) is the most replicated
cross-sectional anomaly; the 52-week-high effect (George–Hwang 2004) is distinct from it; 1-month
reversal is negative; residual momentum (Blitz et al.) is more stable. Low-beta/low-vol earns a
higher risk-adjusted return (Frazzini–Pedersen). None of this is computable from 30 closes.

**Formulas.** `mom12_1 = P(t−21d)/P(t−252d) − 1`; `high52 = P/max252`; `rev1m = P/P(t−21d) − 1`;
`vol252 = σ(daily log returns)·√252`; `beta = cov(r, r_SPX)/var(r_SPX)` over 252 d (replaces the
sector-β table in `moat.ts` for the cost of equity).

**Data.** Extend `fetchDailyCloses` (already keyless, Yahoo chart) to 400 days; persist
`history[]` as-is (schema allows any length ≥ 20). One change unlocks momentum, reversal,
realized vol, beta and the drawdown base rates 10.md needs.

**Placement.** Momentum = a **composite sleeve** (replacing the 52-week-range stand-in); realized vol
and beta = **uncertainty tier** inputs, never label drivers.

## 5. Crowdedness

When `buy share ≥ 0.85`, `price ≥ 0.95 × consensusTarget` and dispersion is low, the upside is
consensus and the downside is not — the "priced-for-perfection" state 4.md's MoS captures from the
model side and this captures from the crowd side. Rule: widen the uncertainty tier one notch (11.md)
and require a larger E for STRONG BUY; do not touch the label directly.

## 6. Worked illustration (today's packs)

- **AMD** — targets $260-$1250, median $625, consensus $598 vs $504 price: `E_Street +19 %`,
  dispersion 1.58 (clamped 1.0), buy share high. Mechanical E (4.md) ≈ −50 %: divergence ≈ 0.7 →
  flag; the correct reading is "the Street and the model disagree by 70 pts, so conviction is low",
  which is exactly the HOLD the desk published — but the report never says so because neither
  number is computed.
- **NVDA** — tight dispersion, buy share high, price below consensus target, mechanical E +21 %:
  divergence small, crowdedness moderate → conviction should be *higher* than AMD's, which the
  current penalty model cannot distinguish (both score 75-80).
- **With a revision series** the two would separate further: the book's semis had the strongest
  upward revisions of the year; a 90-day revision sleeve would put NVDA/AMD in the top decile.

## 8. What to build next (ranked)

1. **Dispersion + divergence + crowdedness into `decide()`** — all fields exist; ~40 lines; fixes
   audit I12/I13. *Highest value per effort in the whole research set.*
2. **Snapshot `analysts`/`estimates` on every capture** (`data/snapshots/`) — enables revisions in
   60-90 days with zero new sources.
3. **400-day price history** via the existing Yahoo closes fetch — unlocks momentum, vol, beta,
   drawdown base rates (10.md) and replaces the sector-β table.
4. **Short interest** from the Yahoo module the peer backfill already parses.
5. **Earnings-surprise history** (FMP/Yahoo) → PEAD sleeve.
6. **Tone scoring** of transcript highlights as a confidence input (last; modest evidence).
