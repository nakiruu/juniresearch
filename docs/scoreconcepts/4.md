# The Intrinsic-Value & Market-Implied-Expectations Engine

## What this document adds

Documents `1.md` and `2.md` value a stock by *comparison*: they score a P/E, an
EV/EBITDA, a PEG, an FCF yield against a sector median or a five-year history and
blend the percentiles into a "cheap vs expensive" reading. That is a relative
screen. It answers "is this multiple high or low versus peers and its own past?"
It never asks what cash the business must actually produce to be worth today's
price, and it never checks whether the price the screen calls "cheap" is cheap
because the market has correctly priced a decline.

This engine asks the inverse question, and it is the deeper one:

> **Given the current price, what growth and margins is the market already paying
> for — and can the business plausibly deliver them?**

This is the "expectations investing" / reverse-DCF frame (Rappaport–Mauboussin;
Greenwald's earnings-power value as the no-growth anchor). The price is treated
as a *hypothesis about the future* that we can decode into an implied operating
path, then test against what five years of statements and the forward estimates
say is achievable. The gap between **implied** and **achievable** is the signal.
A relative multiple can stay "cheap" for years; an expectations gap tells you
which direction the fundamentals have to surprise for the current price to be
right.

The output is not a paragraph of judgment. It is a **mechanical expected upside
`E`, a mechanical bear price, and a fair-value range** that plug directly into
`lib/synth/conviction.ts` in place of — or as a discipline on — the three
analyst-set scenario values that drive the rating today.

This document covers the intrinsic-value / expectations lens only. Moat and
ROIC–WACC durability live in `3.md`; the cross-sectional multi-factor composite
and the earnings-quality / distress gates live in `5.md`; how all of the lenses
combine into one decision lives in `6.md`. Where those overlap this engine, the
reference points here by filename rather than restating them.

## 1. The core object: price as an implied operating path

Every share price is the present value of a cash-flow stream the market expects.
Fix the model structure (discount rate, horizon, terminal growth) and the price
pins down exactly one free variable — the growth rate of owner earnings over the
explicit horizon. Solving for that variable is a **reverse DCF**: we do not
forecast a value and compare it to price; we take the price as given and back out
the assumption embedded in it.

```
Forward DCF:      assumptions  ->  value        (then compare value to price)
Reverse DCF:      price        ->  assumption    (then compare assumption to reality)
```

The reverse direction is more robust for a rating engine because the fragile
input in a forward DCF — the growth rate — becomes the *output*. We are no longer
asserting AMD grows 25%; we are reporting "at $504 the market requires 31%," a
falsifiable statement we can hold against 20% of realized history.

## 3. Owner earnings, not reported earnings

A reverse DCF is only honest if the cash figure it capitalizes is the cash owners
can actually keep. Reported net income and even reported FCF are both wrong for
this purpose, in opposite directions.

**Owner earnings** (Buffett's formulation) starts from operating cash flow and
charges the business for the capital it truly consumes:

```
Owner Earnings =
      Operating Cash Flow
    − Maintenance CapEx                (not total CapEx; growth CapEx is optional)
    − Stock-Based Compensation         (a real cost to owners via dilution)
    ± Normalized Working-Capital swing (strip one-off build/release)
```

Why each adjustment matters, with AMD as the reference:

- **SBC add-back is not free cash.** GAAP adds SBC back to OCF because it is
  non-cash, but it dilutes owners just as surely as writing a check and buying
  the shares back. AMD's buyback is small (`buybacks` −1,316M in FY25 against a
  much larger SBC run-rate), so most SBC leaks into share count. Charging it
  lowers owner earnings from ~8.40B trailing FCF toward ~6.4B, and — critically —
  *raises* the implied growth the price demands (§4), because the market is
  paying for less real cash than the headline FCF suggests.

- **CapEx vs D&A.** AMD is fabless: FY25 CapEx was only 974M on 34.6B of revenue
  (~2.8%), well below EBITDA-vs-operating-income depreciation. FCF conversion is
  therefore high and maintenance CapEx is a small charge. For a capital-heavy
  filer the opposite holds — total CapEx overstates the recurring drain because
  part of it is growth CapEx that a no-growth EPV should exclude. The engine
  must split the two (a fixed maintenance ratio of D&A is a defensible default
  until a better estimate exists).

- **Working capital.** AMD's inventory rose to ~8.5B to pre-build for the Data
  Center ramp (per the Q2'26 transcript). That is a cash outflow depressing
  trailing FCF that will partly reverse. A single-year FCF anchor should be
  normalized across the cycle, not snapped to the most working-capital-heavy
  quarter — otherwise the engine reads a temporary build as permanent.

The rule: **capitalize owner earnings, disclose the bridge from reported FCF, and
run the reverse DCF on both** so the sensitivity to SBC treatment is visible
rather than hidden.

## 4. Reverse DCF — solving for the market-implied growth

### The model

A transparent two-stage FCFE model: owner earnings grow at an unknown constant
`g` for `N` explicit years, then at terminal `g_t` forever, discounted at the
cost of equity `r`. Equity value must equal market cap.

```
                N                                      N
MarketCap  =   Σ   OE₀·(1+g)ᵗ / (1+r)ᵗ   +   OE₀·(1+g) ·(1+g_t)
               t=1                            ─────────────────────
                                              (r − g_t)·(1+r)ᴺ
                └── explicit stage ──┘        └──── terminal value ────┘
```

- `OE₀` = trailing owner earnings (or trailing FCF, run both).
- `r`   = cost of equity. AMD is net cash, so cost of equity ≈ WACC; for a
  levered filer use full WACC and value the enterprise, then bridge by net debt.
- `g_t` = terminal growth, set to a long-run nominal-GDP-like 3%.
- `N`   = 10 explicit years (desk convention).
- `g`   = **the unknown we solve for.**

Because value is monotonic in `g`, a bisection solves it in a few iterations:

```ts
function impliedGrowth(oe0: number, equityValue: number,
                       r = 0.11, gt = 0.03, N = 10): number {
  const value = (g: number) => {
    let v = 0, f = oe0;
    for (let t = 1; t <= N; t++) { f *= 1 + g; v += f / (1 + r) ** t; }
    return v + (f * (1 + gt)) / (r - gt) / (1 + r) ** N;   // + terminal
  };
  let lo = -0.10, hi = 0.80;                 // search band
  for (let i = 0; i < 100; i++) {
    const mid = (lo + hi) / 2;
    if (value(mid) > equityValue) hi = mid; else lo = mid;
  }
  return (lo + hi) / 2;
}
```

### The discount rate (the input that must be added)

`r` is the single most load-bearing assumption and it is **not in the FactPack**.
The defensible proxy, documented per run:

```
Cost of equity  r = R_f + β · ERP
                  ≈ 4.3% (10y UST)  +  1.5 · 4.5% (ERP)   ≈ 11%
```

For a net-cash company `WACC ≈ r`. For a levered one, weight in the after-tax
cost of debt (approximate the pre-tax cost from
`interestExpense / totalDebt`, available via `ttm.interestCoverage` and the
balance sheet). **Until a per-name WACC field is added to the FactPack, hard-code
`r = 11%` as the base and always publish the implied growth across `r ∈
{9%, 11%, 13%}`** so a reader sees how much the conclusion rides on the rate.
This engine should never emit a single implied-growth number without its
rate band.

## 5. The achievable path — what the fundamentals support

The implied growth is only meaningful against a disciplined estimate of what the
business can actually do. Build the achievable path mechanically from the pack:

1. **Historical owner-earnings / FCF CAGR** over the five statement years —
   the base rate.
   ```
   FCF CAGR (FY21→FY25) = (6735 / 3220)^(1/4) − 1 = 20.3%
   Rev CAGR (FY21→FY25) = (34639 / 16434)^(1/4) − 1 = 20.5%
   ```
2. **Near-term forward growth** from `estimates`, which carries analyst
   visibility for the next two years:
   ```
   FY26E revenue growth = 50.87B / 34.64B − 1 = +47%
   FY27E revenue growth = 88.95B / 50.87B − 1 = +75%
   FY26E EPS growth      = 7.62 / 2.65 − 1     = +188%
   FY27E EPS growth      = 15.84 / 7.62 − 1     = +108%
   ```
3. **Fade to a sustainable rate.** Near-term rates like these cannot persist for
   a decade: revenue is scaling off a base that is itself compounding (law of
   large numbers), and the 10-Q risk factors state the industry "is highly
   cyclical and has experienced severe downturns." The engine fades the forward
   rate toward the long-run history and a terminal 3%. A defensible **10-year
   achievable owner-earnings CAGR for AMD lands ~20–22%** — anchored on the 20.3%
   realized history, credited slightly for the visible near-term ramp, and
   penalized for cyclicality and scale.

The achievable number is deliberately conservative and rule-based, not a target.
Its job is to be the yardstick, so the comparison in §6 is not analyst-on-analyst.

## 6. The gap signal

```
Expectations Gap  =  implied growth  −  achievable growth
```

| Gap | Reading | Rating tilt |
|---|---|---|
| Gap ≫ 0 (implied far above achievable) | price pays for growth the business is unlikely to deliver | bearish |
| Gap ≈ 0 | price fairly reflects the achievable path | neutral |
| Gap ≪ 0 (implied below achievable) | price underrates the business; margin of safety present | bullish |

The gap is scale-free and comparable across names, which the raw multiples in
`1.md`/`2.md` are not: a 30x P/E means nothing without the growth behind it, but
"the price needs 31% and the company can do 21%" is directly interpretable and
directly comparable to a name whose price needs 6% against an achievable 9%.

**AMD:** implied ~31% (owner-earnings basis ~35%) vs achievable ~21% → gap of
roughly **+10 percentage points a year, for ten years**. The price embeds a
decade of near-best-case execution with essentially no cyclical interruption.

## 7. Forward intrinsic value & margin of safety

The reverse DCF tells you what the price assumes; the forward pass turns the
achievable path into a **fair-value range**, which is what `conviction.ts`
consumes. Hold the model of §4 fixed and vary only `g` to trace bear / base /
bull. This keeps the range internally consistent — the three cases differ by one
lever, not three unrelated analyst guesses.

| Scenario | Growth assumption | Rationale | Implied FV / share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** | implied ÷ 2 = 15.7% | required growth halves (a cyclical air-pocket) | **$166** |
| **Base** | ≈ achievable = 20% | the realized-history rate persists | **$226** |
| **Bull** | 28% | near the market-implied rate, just short of it | **$398** |
| *(marker)* | *31.4% = implied* | *the growth priced in today* | *$504 = price* |

(Model: `OE₀` = 8.40B trailing FCF, `r` = 11%, `g_t` = 3%, `N` = 10; verified by
the bisection above — 31.4% reproduces the $504 price exactly.)

**Margin of safety** is the base-case discount to price:

```
MoS = 1 − (Base FV / Price) = 1 − (226 / 504) = −55%
```

A negative margin of safety means the base case sits *below* the price: you are
paying up for the bull case. Contrast a name whose base FV is $130 at a $100
price (+30% MoS) — same rating machinery, opposite verdict.

### Sensitivity (mandatory output, never suppressed)

The point estimate is sensitive to `r` and `g_t`; the engine must publish the
grid, not a single number. Base case (`g` = 20%), FV per share:

| | `g_t` = 2% | `g_t` = 3% | `g_t` = 4% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `r` = 9% | $287 | $322 | $371 |
| `r` = 11% | $208 | $226 | $248 |
| `r` = 13% | $160 | $170 | $181 |

And the implied 10-year growth needed to justify $504.20, by rate:

| Discount rate | Implied 10y FCF growth |
|---|---:|
| `r` = 9% | 26.1% |
| `r` = 11% | 31.4% |
| `r` = 13% | 36.1% |

Across the whole plausible band the required growth is **26–36%** and the base
case FV never reaches the price. The conclusion — AMD is priced for exceptional,
sustained growth — is robust to the discount-rate choice, even though the exact
fair value is not.

## 8. Producing and disciplining `E` and `R`

### The clean plug: the engine emits `ScenarioIn[]`

`conviction.ts` already consumes exactly the shape this engine produces. From
`lib/format.ts`:

```ts
interface ScenarioIn { name: string; driver: string; impliedPrice: number; probability: number; }
```

`computeConviction` takes `ScenarioIn[]` + price, computes `fairValue` as the
probability-weighted `impliedPrice`, sets `E = upside(fairValue, price)`, finds
the `/bear/i` scenario for `D = (price − bearImplied)/price`, and returns
`R = E/D`. **Nothing in `conviction.ts` needs to change.** This engine simply
becomes an upstream *producer* of the three scenarios that today are set by hand:

```ts
// intrinsic.ts — new module; conviction.ts unchanged.
function mechanicalScenarios(f: FactPack, r = 0.11, gt = 0.03, N = 10): ScenarioIn[] {
  const oe0    = ownerEarnings(f);                 // §3
  const equity = f.quote.marketCap;
  const gImpl  = impliedGrowth(oe0, equity, r, gt, N);   // §4
  const fv     = (g: number) => dcfValue(oe0, g, r, gt, N) / f.quote.sharesOutstanding;
  return [
    { name: "Bear (implied growth halves)", driver: `g=${(gImpl/2*100).toFixed(0)}%`,
      impliedPrice: fv(gImpl / 2),      probability: 0.25 },
    { name: "Base (achievable growth)",     driver: `g=${(achievable(f)*100).toFixed(0)}%`,
      impliedPrice: fv(achievable(f)),  probability: 0.50 },
    { name: "Bull (near market-implied)",   driver: `g=${((gImpl*0.9)*100).toFixed(0)}%`,
      impliedPrice: fv(gImpl * 0.9),    probability: 0.25 },
  ];
}
```

Feed the result straight in:

```ts
const scen = mechanicalScenarios(factpack);
const conviction = computeConviction(scen, factpack.quote.price);   // E, D, R — all mechanical
const label      = deriveLabel(conviction, desk.rating);            // unchanged thresholds
```

Now `E` is not asserted. The bear is not a formality — it is the value if the
required growth halves, which mechanically satisfies the `desk.json`
`bearFloor: 0.15` for AMD (bear $166 is 67% below price). The base is the
achievable path. `R` is grounded because both numerator and denominator come from
the same model.

### Reconciling the mechanical `E` with the analyst scenario `E`

The engine should **discipline** the analyst, not silently replace him. Run both:

```
E_mechanical  = prob-weighted mechanical FV / price − 1
E_analyst     = prob-weighted analyst FV     / price − 1
```

**AMD:** the mechanical prob-weighted FV is `0.25·166 + 0.50·226 + 0.25·398 =
$254`, so `E_mechanical = 254/504 − 1 = −50%`. The Street consensus target is
$598 (`E ≈ +19%`), median $625 (`+24%`). That is a **~70-point divergence** —
the single most important thing the engine can surface.

Handling rule (mirrors the one-way `conservativeNotch` already in
`conviction.ts`):

1. **Never let the raw mechanical `E` stamp an extreme on its own.** A −50% `E`
   maps to STRONG SELL. But that figure is driven by anchoring on *trailing* FCF
   during a 100%+ earnings inflection — a known DCF pitfall. The engine reports
   it, flagged, and does not auto-rate on it.
2. **Use the mechanical `E` as a ceiling, not an override.** When
   `E_mechanical ≪ E_analyst`, the mechanical view *caps* how bullish the rating
   may be: pull the analyst's label at least one bucket toward HOLD. This is
   exactly the discipline that took the desk's AMD from BUY to HOLD (the standing
   `R ≈ 0.37` read). The engine formalizes what the analyst did by hand.
3. **Emit a `divergence` flag** whenever `|E_mechanical − E_analyst| > 0.25` so a
   human sees *why* the two disagree — here, "trailing FCF vs a steep forward
   ramp; price capitalizes FY27+ earnings the trailing DCF cannot see." A large
   divergence is information, not an error to be smoothed over.
4. **Report the gap and the required growth in the judgment**, so the rating
   rests on "the price needs 31%, history says 20%" rather than on any single
   fair-value point.

The net effect for AMD: the engine does not fabricate a STRONG SELL that ignores
real momentum and Street support, and it does not rubber-stamp a BUY that ignores
a −55% margin of safety. It lands on **HOLD — priced for perfection, low margin
of safety** — which is the defensible call and the one the desk actually reached.

## 9. Worked example — AMD (FactPack `0000002488-26-000123`, price $504.20)

**Step 1 — owner-earnings base.** Trailing FCF = `fcfYield × marketCap` =
`0.01022 × 822.1B ≈ 8.40B`. SBC-adjusted owner earnings ≈ 6.4B (SBC leaks to
dilution; buyback only −1.3B). Run both.

**Step 2 — reverse DCF (`r` = 11%, `g_t` = 3%, `N` = 10).**
Implied 10y growth = **31.4%** on trailing FCF, **35.4%** on owner earnings.
Across `r ∈ {9, 11, 13}%`: **26–36%**.

**Step 3 — achievable path.** History: FCF CAGR 20.3%, revenue CAGR 20.5%.
Forward Street: FY26E revenue +47%, FY27E +75% (EPS +188% then +108%) — steep but
fading, against a "highly cyclical" industry (10-Q). Disciplined 10y achievable
owner-earnings CAGR ≈ **21%**.

**Step 4 — the gap.** `31% − 21% ≈ +10 pts/yr` → the price embeds a decade of
near-best-case execution with no cyclical break. **Bearish tilt.**

**Step 5 — fair-value range & `E`.**

| | Bear (15.7%) | Base (20%) | Bull (28%) | Prob-wtd |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FV / share | $166 | $226 | $398 | **$254** |
| Weight | 0.25 | 0.50 | 0.25 | |

`E_mechanical = 254/504 − 1 = −50%`; margin of safety `= −55%`.

**Step 6 — reconcile.** Street `E ≈ +19%` (target $598). Divergence ~70 pts →
flagged: trailing-FCF DCF vs a forward ramp the market is capitalizing. The raw
−50% is **not** stamped as STRONG SELL; it **caps** the analyst BUY down to
**HOLD**. The judgment reports "at $504 AMD must compound FCF ~31%/yr for a
decade; twenty years of history and the cyclical risk factor support ~21%; the
base case is worth ~$226 and there is no margin of safety." Rating: **HOLD**,
consistent with the desk's standing AMD call.

This is the value of the engine: same facts as the multiple-percentile screen
(which would flag AMD's 128x P/E as "expensive" but could not say by how much or
against what), turned into a specific, testable claim about required growth and a
grounded `E`.

## 10. Why this is deeper than the multiple-percentile valuation in `1.md`/`2.md`

| | Multiple-percentile (`1.md`/`2.md`) | This engine |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Is the multiple high vs sector/history? | What must the business deliver to justify the price? |
| Growth | An input scored in a separate pillar | The *solved output* the price implies |
| Anchor | Peer/own-history percentile | Discounted owner-earnings cash |
| "Cheap" trap | A value-trap screens cheap and stays cheap | A value trap shows implied ≪ achievable *only if* fundamentals confirm — cross-checked |
| Comparability | 30x P/E means little without context | "needs 31%, can do 21%" is directly comparable across names |
| Output for the rating | A 0–100 score blended with others | A mechanical `E`, bear price, and FV range that *are* the scenarios |
| Failure mode | Silent — a high multiple with high growth looks bad | Explicit — the gap and sensitivity grid are published |

The percentile screen and this engine are complements, not substitutes: the
screen is a fast cross-sectional filter (its natural home is the composite in
`5.md`), while this engine is the intrinsic anchor that grounds `E` so the
reward/risk rule in `conviction.ts` is not resting on three free-hand numbers.

## 11. Limitations & required new data

- **Discount rate is exogenous and decisive.** `r` is not in the FactPack, and
  the implied growth swings 26%→36% across a 9–13% band. *Required:* a per-name
  WACC / cost-of-equity field (risk-free, beta, ERP, capital weights). Until
  then, `r = 11%` base with a mandatory published rate band.
- **SBC is not a separate FactPack row.** It sits inside `operatingCashFlow`, so
  owner earnings cannot be computed exactly. *Required:* a
  `cashflow[stockBasedComp]` row. Directionally, ignoring SBC *understates* the
  implied growth, so the engine errs optimistic if it uses raw FCF.
- **Trailing-FCF anchor mis-times inflections.** For a company ramping earnings
  100%+ (AMD), a trailing base makes the price look extreme because the DCF
  cannot see the near-term ramp the market is capitalizing. Mitigations: run
  owner-earnings *and* a forward-normalized base off `estimates`, and rely on the
  *gap* and the *required-growth* read (both robust) rather than the raw `E`
  point estimate.
- **Cyclicality and terminal assumptions.** A single-point `g_t` and a smooth
  fade cannot capture a semiconductor down-cycle. The bear scenario ("implied
  growth halves") is the crude proxy; a proper cyclical version would normalize
  owner earnings over a full cycle before capitalizing. Cross-reference the
  distress/earnings-quality gates in `5.md` and the moat-durability adjustment to
  terminal growth in `3.md`.
- **Two-stage FCFE is a simplification.** No explicit reinvestment-rate link
  (`g = reinvestment × ROIC`), no separate margin trajectory. That link is where
  this engine should meet `3.md`'s ROIC–WACC work: terminal growth is only
  credible up to `ROIC × reinvestment`, and only durable while ROIC exceeds WACC.
- **Non-cash-generative names.** For pre-FCF issuers (owner earnings ≤ 0) the
  reverse DCF has no solution; the engine must fall back to a revenue/margin-path
  model or hand off to the relative screen, and flag that it abstained.

## 12. Reference interface

```ts
interface IntrinsicResult {
  ownerEarnings: number;          // §3, with reported-FCF bridge disclosed
  impliedGrowth: number;          // §4, on owner earnings
  impliedGrowthBand: [number, number, number];   // r = 9 / 11 / 13%
  achievableGrowth: number;       // §5
  gap: number;                    // impliedGrowth − achievableGrowth  (the signal)
  fairValue: { bear: number; base: number; bull: number };   // §7, per share
  marginOfSafety: number;         // 1 − base/price
  scenarios: ScenarioIn[];        // §8 — fed straight into computeConviction()
  eMechanical: number;            // upside(prob-weighted FV, price)
  eAnalyst: number | null;        // from analysts.consensusTarget, if present
  divergence: number | null;      // |eMechanical − eAnalyst|
  ratingCap: RatingLabel | null;  // one-way ceiling applied when divergence is large
  discountRate: number;           // r used — always reported
  flags: string[];                // "trailing-FCF anchor", "SBC estimated", "no FCF — abstained", ...
}
```

The engine's contract with the rest of the system is narrow and testable: it
produces `scenarios`, `eMechanical`, and `ratingCap`. `conviction.ts` consumes
`scenarios` unchanged to yield `(E, R)` and `deriveLabel`; `6.md` decides how the
`ratingCap` and the gap combine with the moat (`3.md`) and cross-sectional
(`5.md`) lenses into the final published rating.
