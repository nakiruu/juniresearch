# 7 — Adversarial audit of the scoring pipeline (branch `worktree-scoring-gates-prototype`)

Audit date 2026-09-22. Scope: `lib/synth/{gates,intrinsic,moat,composite,decide}.ts`, their wiring
(`scripts/synth-build.ts`, `lib/synth/merge.ts`, `lib/report.schema.ts`), the data path
(`lib/facts/{schema,validate,enrich}.ts`, `scripts/backfill-*.ts`, `scripts/facts-enrich.ts`) and the
tests. Every finding below was reproduced against the real packs in `data/facts/` (T, CRWV, ORCL ×2,
KTOS, AMD, and synthetic edge inputs) — not inferred from reading alone. Nothing was fixed here.

## Executive summary

1. The system is **safe by construction** — every layer is caps-only/advisory and `SAFE_DEFAULTS` make
   `decide()` return today's E/R label, so no published rating is currently wrong *because of* this code.
2. The **intrinsic-value layer has two genuine correctness defects** that only show on cheap or
   shrinking names: the scenario sort reassigns the 50 % "base" probability to the wrong growth
   assumption (C1), and a 3 % growth **floor** manufactures a margin of safety for a declining business
   (C2). AT&T reads MoS **+22 %** for these two reasons alone.
3. The moat engine will emit a **confident verdict from a single comparable year** on a young issuer
   (CRWV) (I1), and **its main modulation lever — the bear-depth floor — is computed but never
   applied** anywhere (I11): 3.md's Lever 1 is unwired.
4. Sector classification silently routes **REITs (SIC 6798) to "industrial"**, where the leverage
   and current-ratio gates will misfire exactly as they did on utilities before sector-awareness (I2).
5. Policy constants (conviction penalties, the composite tail threshold, rf/ERP) are **hard-coded in
   four places** rather than `desk.json`; 6.md specified the opposite (I3, I4).
6. Two 4.md/6.md mechanisms that the data already supports are **not built**: the Street-vs-model
   divergence flag (`analysts.consensusTarget` exists) and analyst-target dispersion in conviction (I12, I13).
7. The Growth sleeve's logistic **saturates at ≈1.0 for every hypergrowth name**, so the composite
   cannot rank AMD vs NVDA vs CRWV on growth (I6).
8. Tests are green (783) but several **load live, re-enrichable packs as fixtures** — `facts:enrich`
   can silently move them (I9); `validate.ts` never checks `goodwill.length` (I10).
9. The "rare and severe" gate outcome (1/21 caps) is **in-sample on the book it was tuned against**;
   the thresholds are economically motivated but have no out-of-sample check (§5).
10. Fix order: C1, C2, I11 (they change numbers the desk could act on), then I2/I1 (silent misfires on
    the next new sector/young issuer), then the config consolidation.

## 1. Critical

### C1 — `intrinsic.ts:134-139`: the price sort reassigns scenario probabilities to the wrong economics

`intrinsicRead` builds three growth candidates — bear = implied/2, **base = achievable**, bull =
0.9·implied — then **sorts them by resulting price** and assigns `[bear, base, bull]` positionally with
fixed probabilities 0.25/0.50/0.25. The sort was added so a cheap stock (implied < achievable) still
orders bull ≥ base ≥ bear. But it also moves the *probability mass*: for a cheap name the achievable
case (the economic base) becomes the highest price and is labelled **Bull at 25 %**, while
implied/2 becomes **Base at 50 %**.

Reproduced on T (`0000732717-26-000297`): implied −5.1 %, achievable 3.0 %; emitted scenarios are
`Bear: ~−5 %` (= 0.9·implied), `Base: ~−3 %` (= implied/2), `Bull: ~+3 %` (= achievable). The
probability-weighted E is therefore dominated by half-of-implied-growth, not by the achievable path
4.md defines as the base. On an expensive name (AMD) the order coincides with the intent, which is why
the tests passed.

**Failure scenario:** any name with implied < achievable — exactly the "cheap" tail where the reverse
DCF is supposed to add value — gets a mis-weighted E and a semantically inverted driver text.

**Fix:** keep the probability attached to the economic role. Anchor base = achievable (0.50); define
bear = min(implied/2, achievable − δ) and bull = max(0.9·implied, achievable + δ) with δ derived from
the implied/achievable gap, so ordering is guaranteed without re-labelling. Add a test with
implied < achievable asserting `Base.driver` names the achievable rate.

### C2 — `intrinsic.ts:108-117`: the 3 % growth floor inflates shrinking businesses

`achievableGrowth` returns `Math.min(0.35, Math.max(0.03, g))`. A declining FCF or revenue CAGR is
clamped **up** to +3 %. T: FCF 25.4 → 19.4 B (≈ −6.5 %/yr), revenue 134 → 126 B, yet the base case is
valued at +3 % perpetual growth → base FV $32 vs $27 price → **MoS +22 %, mechanical E +33 %**.
A structurally declining business reads as the cheapest name in the book.

**Failure scenario:** any secular decliner (telcos, legacy media, tobacco-style) gets a bullish
intrinsic read and a positive conviction contribution.

**Fix:** floor at a negative bound (e.g. `max(−0.10, g)`), or cap achievable at the observed revenue
trend when both FCF and revenue CAGRs are negative; surface a `declining` flag; test on T.

## 2. Important

### I1 — `moat.ts:143` + verdict logic: a confident verdict from one comparable year

`comparableWindow` returns the *latest* structural break; on CRWV (3 fiscal years) it returns index 2,
leaving **one** comparable year. `moatRead` then computes a median of one spread, a "stability" share
from one point, a trend from one point, and emits `NARROW / STABLE` with a 0.20 bear floor and no
low-confidence marker; `incrementalRoic` is `null` and the rule silently treats it as NONE.

**Fix:** require ≥ 3 comparable years for a Width/Trend verdict; otherwise return
`width: "INSUFFICIENT"` (or NONE + `confidence: "low"`), skip the bear-floor lever, and surface the
reason. Mirrors the young-issuer handling 3.md §8 already asks for.

### I2 — `gates.ts:112-121`: SIC gaps route leverage-heavy sectors to industrial gates

`sectorFromSic` maps 6000-6199, 6211, 6300-6499 → financial and 4900-4999 → utility; everything
else → industrial. Probed: **REIT 6798 → industrial**, holding companies 6719 → industrial, asset
managers 6282 → industrial. A REIT carries utility-like leverage and a sub-1 current ratio by
design; the industrial distress tiers (`assessDistress`, :164-190) would cap it at HOLD/SELL for
being a REIT — the same false positive NEE produced before utilities were special-cased.

**Fix:** map 6798 (and 6795/6796 trusts) to the utility branch (leverage/current-ratio not
applicable; coverage/runway still meaningful) and 6719 to financial; add a `sectorFromSic` test
for each. The ticker map (:123) should stay a fallback only.

### I3 — macro constants duplicated in four places

rf 4.3 % / ERP 4.5 % live in `moat.ts:49-50`, `scripts/synth-build.ts:55`,
`scripts/intrinsic-preview.ts:15`, `scripts/decide-preview.ts:48`. 6.md §3.4 puts every policy number
in `desk.json`. A future desk edit will change one and not the others; the pipeline's discount rate
would then diverge from the preview's.

**Fix:** export a single `MACRO` (or add `desk.capitalMarkets`) and import it everywhere.

### I4 — `decide.ts:80, 90-99`: hard-coded policy constants, and a penalty that punishes correct HOLDs

Conviction penalties (−25/−15/−20/−10/−10/−10/−5), tiers (70/45) and the composite tail threshold
(≥ 50 / ≤ 50) are literals; 6.md specifies `desk.rating.conviction.weights`. Separately,
line 92 subtracts 20 whenever `sign(MoS) ≠ sign(E)` **regardless of label** — a HOLD is often the
*correct synthesis* of exactly that disagreement (AMD: E +12 %, MoS −60 % → HOLD, and still −20).

**Fix:** move the constants to `desk.json` with the current values as defaults; apply the
MoS/E-sign penalty only when the label is not HOLD.

### I5 — two different labels are in play and neither output says so

`decide()` never receives the author's published label: `proposed` is `deriveLabel`, and every
conviction/corroboration step reasons about the *derived* label. Meanwhile `synth-build.ts:88`
computes the gate advisory against `valid.rating.label` (the **author's** label, which may sit one
conservative notch below derived). `rating.decision.proposed` and `rating.label` can therefore
legitimately differ with no field explaining why.

**Fix:** pass the author label into `decide` (`inputs.published`), persist both, compute `gateBinds`
and the disagreement penalties against the published label, and add a `reason` when they differ.

### I6 — `composite.ts:105-110`: the Growth sleeve saturates

`logistic((g − 0.08) / 0.12)` is ≈ 0.9999 for any g above ~60 %. CRWV (0.9999), NVDA, AMD and CBRS
are indistinguishable on growth, and the constants 8 %/12 % are unexplained. With a 0.7 weight this
pushes every hypergrowth name's composite up by the same amount.

**Fix:** rank growth against the peer set when peer growth is captured (it is not today) or against
the company's own history; until then cap the map at ~0.95 and widen the scale, and document the
constants.

### I7 — Value sleeve: small-n and an un-bounded outlier

`valueSleeve` requires ≥ 3 valid peers per metric, so with 4 peers one bad datum is 25 % of the
cross-section. Rank is robust to *magnitude* (ASML's Yahoo EV/EBITDA of 2707× becomes a tiny yield
and just sits at the bottom), but it still shifts AMD's EBITDA-yield rank by one slot. The composite
weights (1.0/0.7/0.7/0.4, :131-134) are also unexplained.

**Fix:** sanity-bound multiples before ranking (drop EV/EBITDA > 200 or yields < 0.05 %), record
`droppedPeers`, and lower confidence when < 4 valid. Document or config the weights.

### I8 — the gate advisory is dropped on a failing build

`synth-build.ts` calls `fail(errors, warnings, ...)` before the gate advisory is computed (:88-89),
so a build that fails validation never shows the fundamental warning, and the `errors.txt`
re-prompt payload never carries `decision.reasons`/`advisories`. The author never sees conviction.

**Fix:** compute `gateWarning` (and a one-line decision summary) before the first `fail()` and
include them in the warnings passed to it; consider writing `decision.reasons` into the errors file
under the warnings heading.

### I9 — tests use live, re-enrichable packs as fixtures

`gates/moat/intrinsic/composite/decide.test.ts` load `data/facts/<T>/<acc>.json`. `facts:enrich`
(and the backfills) rewrite those files with *current* Yahoo multiples and SEC goodwill. The
composite tests assert only ranges, but `classifySector(BAC) === "financial"` relies on the
backfilled `sic`, and the moat tests' AMD verdict relies on the backfilled `goodwill` being
sub-WACC ex-goodwill. A future enrich run can change test outcomes without any code change.

**Fix:** copy the packs used in tests into `lib/synth/__fixtures__/` (the repo already has this
pattern for `avgo-golden-judgment.json`) and load those.

### I10 — `lib/facts/validate.ts` has no goodwill check

`goodwill` is an optional array with no length constraint (schema) and `validate.ts` never
references it (grep: 0). `alignGoodwill` guarantees alignment only when the array was produced by
`enrich.ts`; a hand-edited or partially written pack passes validation with a mis-aligned series and
the moat engine indexes it by fiscal year silently.

**Fix:** in `validateFactPack`, if `goodwill` is present require `goodwill.length === fiscalYears.length`.

### I11 — `moat.ts`: the bear-depth floor is computed but never applied

`moatBearFloor` → `bearFloor` is persisted nowhere and consumed by nothing: `decide()` reads only
`width/trend/contingent`, `validate-judgment.ts` enforces only the desk's flat 15 % floor, and
`conviction.ts` is untouched. 3.md §5 Lever 1 (moat sets the minimum bear depth, which moves R) is
the engine's primary economic channel and it is unwired. Lever 2's "no BUY into an Eroding moat"
is likewise only a −15 conviction penalty, not a cap.

**Fix (policy-gated like the others):** in `decide`, when `policy.applyMoatFloor` is on, recompute
`D' = max(D, bearFloor)` and `R' = E/D'` before `deriveLabel`; surface the recomputed R in the
decision block; optionally add the floor to `ratingIssues` as a warning.

### I12 — 4.md §8's Street-vs-model divergence is not built, though the data exists

`analysts.consensusTarget/medianTarget` are in every pack; `intrinsicRead` computes a mechanical E.
4.md specifies `eAnalyst`, `divergence = |E_mech − E_analyst|`, a `divergence` flag at > 0.25 and a
one-way `ratingCap`. None of it exists; `decide` uses only the *sign* of MoS.

**Fix:** add `eAnalyst`/`divergence` to `IntrinsicResult`, persist them, and feed `divergence`
into conviction (a large divergence is information, per 4.md).

### I13 — analyst-target dispersion is available and unused in conviction

6.md §4.1 names target dispersion `(high − low)/median` as the first conviction input; AMD's is
1.58 (clamped 1.0 → maximal penalty in the spec). `decide.ts` has no such term. Conviction today is
purely cross-layer disagreement + completeness.

**Fix:** add `dispersionPenalty` from `analysts` (it is in every pack) with a desk weight.

- **M2 `moat.ts:126`** — `sectorHurdle` is now unreachable in the default path (`buildWacc` always

- **M3 `intrinsic.ts:58,149`** — `impliedGrowthBand` is computed but not persisted; 4.md §7 calls the
  sensitivity band a *mandatory* output.
- **M4 `intrinsic.ts:99`** — `ownerEarningsBase` prefers the vendor's quote-time `fcfYield × marketCap`
  over the statement FCF row; the two ORCL packs (10-Q and 10-K) share one `fcfYield`, mixing
  price-date and filing-date data. Prefer the statement row, fall back to the yield.
- **M5 `composite.ts`** — Momentum is 52-week-range-only (no 12-1, no reversal) and Low-Vol is absent;
  its 0.4 weight is the only acknowledgement. Quality returns `null` silently when history < 3.
- **M6 `decide.ts`** — `policy.maxNotches` is declared, documented and never read.
- **M7 `enrich.ts`** — one Yahoo crumb per run with no refresh/retry; fine for 65 peers, fragile for a
  larger book. `backfill-*.ts` duplicate the fetch logic (historical one-offs; keep or delete).
- **M8 `gates.ts:140-146`** — precedence is SIC → free-text sector → ticker map. Correct, but if a
  Yahoo `assetProfile.sector` ("Financial Services") is ever persisted *without* SIC, V-like payment
  networks would be mis-classed financial. Keep SIC first; add a test.
- **M9** — the report UI renders none of `rating.gate` / `rating.decision`; the work is invisible to a
  reader.
- **M10** — CRLF/LF warnings on every write (Windows autocrlf); cosmetic, but a `.gitattributes`

## 5. Calibration and overfitting notes

- Gate tiers (runway < 1 y / < 2 y, ND/EBITDA > 6, coverage < 1) are economically motivated and
  were **not** tuned numerically; but the "1 of 21 caps" result is measured on the same book the
  sector rules were derived from. There is no held-out check. Before hard enforcement, run the gates
  over a broader universe (e.g. the S&P 100 via the free source) and confirm the cap rate stays in
  the ~5-15 % band 6.md asks for.
- Unexplained constants that *would* move outputs if changed: composite weights (1.0/0.7/0.7/0.4),
  Growth logistic (8 %, 12 %), composite tail (50), conviction penalties, tiers (70/45), moat width
  cut-offs (+8 pts, 80 %, CoV 0.35, 40 % GM), goodwill materiality (15 %), structural break (50 %).
  All should be `desk.json` with the current values as defaults so a change is a reviewable policy
  edit (6.md §7.1 #5).
- The one enforcement-sensitive name (CRWV → SELL) is a genuine solvency read, not an artefact; but
  its 3-year history also makes it the name with the least support for a moat/composite verdict (I1).

## 6. Recommended fix order

1. **C1, C2** — intrinsic scenario roles and the growth floor (they change E for cheap/declining names).
2. **I11** — wire the moat bear floor behind a policy knob; it is the layer's purpose.
3. **I2, I1** — REIT/holding SIC routing; minimum comparable years. Both are silent misfires on the
   *next* company, not this book.
4. **I12, I13** — Street divergence and target dispersion: data already present, small code.
5. **I3, I4, I5** — one config source for macro/policy; pass the published label into `decide`.
6. **I6, I7** — Growth saturation and Value bounds.
7. **I8, I9, I10** — advisory on failing builds; frozen fixtures; goodwill length validation.
8. Minors, then the report UI (M9), which is what makes any of this visible.
