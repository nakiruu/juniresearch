# 1-Year Fundamental Stock Rating System

## Overview

This system rates a stock for a **one-year investment horizon** using a 0–100 composite score.

The central question is:

> Given the company's expected fundamentals, competitive position, balance sheet, valuation, and plausible catalysts over the next 12 months, how attractive is the stock relative to its risk?

The system separates **business quality** from **stock attractiveness**. A high-quality company can receive a lower rating when valuation is excessive, while a mediocre company can receive a higher rating when expectations are sufficiently low.

---

## Rating Scale

| Score | Rating |
|---:|---|
| 85–100 | **STRONG BUY** |
| 70–84.99 | **BUY** |
| 55–69.99 | **HOLD** |
| 40–54.99 | **SELL** |
| 0–39.99 | **STRONG SELL** |

---

# 1. Valuation — 20%

Valuation measures how much the market is paying relative to the company's earnings, cash flow, growth, and estimated intrinsic value.

### Core Metrics

- P/E
- EV/EBITDA
- Price/FCF
- PEG
- DCF implied return/value
- Sector-relative valuation
- Historical valuation

### Proposed Valuation Formula

```text
Valuation Score =
    25% P/E
  + 20% EV/EBITDA
  + 20% P/FCF
  + 20% PEG
  + 15% DCF
```

Each component is normalized to 0–100.

Valuation should be **sector-relative**. A P/E of 30 may be expensive for a bank but reasonable for a rapidly growing software company.

A useful normalization approach is:

```text
P/E Score = PercentileRank(Sector P/E / Company P/E)
```

The same principle can be applied to other valuation multiples.

---

# 2. Earnings & FCF Growth — 20%

This measures the company's historical and forward growth profile.

### Core Metrics

- Revenue YoY growth
- Revenue CAGR
- EPS YoY growth
- EPS CAGR
- EBITDA growth
- FCF growth
- Forward EPS growth
- Margin expansion
- Growth acceleration

### Growth Acceleration

```text
Growth Acceleration =
    Current Growth - Prior Growth
```

Acceleration should be rewarded because a company moving from 8% to 15% growth is fundamentally different from a company declining from 20% to 15%.

### Proposed Growth Formula

```text
Growth Score =
    25% EPS Growth
  + 20% Revenue Growth
  + 20% FCF Growth
  + 15% Forward EPS Growth
  + 10% Growth Acceleration
  + 10% Margin Expansion
```

---

# 3. Business Quality — 15%

This evaluates the underlying quality of the business rather than the stock price.

## Profitability

- ROIC
- ROE
- ROA
- Gross margin
- Operating margin
- FCF margin

## Stability

- Revenue volatility
- EPS volatility
- FCF volatility
- Margin stability

## Capital Efficiency

ROIC should receive greater emphasis than ROE because ROE can be artificially increased through leverage.

```text
ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital
```

### Proposed Quality Formula

```text
Quality Score =
    25% ROIC
  + 15% FCF Margin
  + 15% Operating Margin
  + 10% Gross Margin
  + 10% ROE
  + 15% Margin Stability
  + 10% Revenue Stability
```

---

# 4. Competitive Moat — 15%

The moat score measures how difficult it is for competitors to replicate or displace the company's economic advantages.

A moat should not be a simple yes/no variable.

## Moat Components

### Switching Costs — 15%

How difficult is it for customers to leave?

### Network Effects — 15%

Does the product become more valuable as adoption increases?

### Cost Advantage — 15%

Can the company structurally produce or distribute at lower cost?

### Intangible Assets — 15%

- Patents
- Brand
- Licensing
- Proprietary technology
- Regulatory approvals

### Distribution Advantage — 10%

Can competitors easily replicate the company's distribution?

### Economies of Scale — 10%

Does the company's size itself create an advantage?

### Customer Lock-In — 10%

Examples:

- Long-term contracts
- Integration costs
- Recurring relationships
- Data dependence

### Competitive Intensity — 10%

This component works inversely.

```text
Competitive Intensity Score =
    100 - Competition Risk
```

Consider:

- Industry concentration
- Number of credible competitors
- Pricing pressure
- Margin trends
- New entrants

---

# 5. Financial Health — 10%

Financial health functions both as a quality measure and a risk-control mechanism.

### Core Metrics

- Net debt / EBITDA
- Debt / FCF
- Interest coverage
- Current ratio
- Cash / debt
- FCF / debt
- Altman Z-score
- Share dilution

### Proposed Formula

```text
Financial Health =
    25% Liquidity
  + 25% Leverage
  + 20% Interest Coverage
  + 20% FCF / Debt
  + 10% Dilution
```

### Dilution

Explicitly penalize growth accompanied by excessive share issuance.

```text
Dilution Penalty =
    max(0, Change in Shares Outstanding)
```

A company growing EPS through genuine operating growth and buybacks should be treated differently from a company growing revenue while issuing large quantities of stock.

---

# 6. Forward Expectations & Revisions — 10%

The market does not only care whether a company is good.

It cares whether the company is likely to perform **better or worse than what the market already expects**.

### Metrics

- 30-day EPS estimate revisions
- 90-day EPS estimate revisions
- Revenue estimate revisions
- EBITDA estimate revisions
- FCF estimate revisions
- Analyst estimate dispersion

### EPS Revision Formula

```text
Revision Score =
    (Current EPS Estimate - Previous EPS Estimate)
    / Previous EPS Estimate
```

Calculate this over multiple periods, such as 30 and 90 days.

### Estimate Dispersion

A narrow range such as:

```text
$100 / $102 / $103 / $105
```

indicates less uncertainty than:

```text
$60 / $90 / $120 / $150
```

Higher dispersion should reduce confidence.

---

# 7. Catalysts & Risks — 5%

A one-year model needs to account for events that could materially affect the stock over the next 12 months.

## Potential Catalysts

- New products
- New markets
- Capacity expansion
- Margin expansion
- Cost reductions
- Regulatory approvals
- Buybacks
- Debt reduction
- Acquisitions
- Major contracts
- Industry cycle improvements

## Potential Risks

- Regulatory changes
- Commodity exposure
- Customer concentration
- Competitive threats
- Recession sensitivity
- Refinancing requirements
- Litigation
- Product concentration

Conceptually:

```text
Catalyst/Risk Score =
    Catalyst Score - Risk Score
```

Normalize the result to 0–100.

---

# 8. Price / Market Confirmation — 5%

Technical factors should have a small weight rather than dominating the fundamental system.

## Metrics

### Relative Strength

Compare the stock against:

- S&P 500
- Sector ETF
- Industry peers

### Trend

- Price > 200DMA
- Price > 50DMA
- 50DMA > 200DMA

### Momentum

- 3-month return
- 6-month return
- 12-month return

This answers:

> Is the market currently confirming the fundamental thesis?

---

# 9. Master Score

The complete score is:

```text
Score =
    20% Valuation
  + 20% Growth
  + 15% Business Quality
  + 15% Competitive Moat
  + 10% Financial Health
  + 10% Forward Expectations
  +  5% Catalysts/Risks
  +  5% Market Confirmation
```

Each factor is scored from 0–100.

Therefore:

```text
Final Score = Σ(Factor Score × Factor Weight)
```

---

# 10. Expected 1-Year Return

The rating should not rely exclusively on a quality score.

Calculate an expected one-year return using multiple valuation approaches.

```text
Expected Return =
    w1 DCF Return
  + w2 Multiple Return
  + w3 Earnings Return
  + w4 FCF Return
  + w5 Catalyst Return
```

---

# 11. Bull / Base / Bear Scenario Model

Construct three scenarios.

## Bull Case

Assumes favorable execution and stronger-than-expected fundamentals.

## Base Case

Assumes management/consensus expectations are approximately achieved.

## Bear Case

Assumes weaker growth, margin pressure, valuation compression, or material execution problems.

Example:

```text
Current Price: $100

Bear Case: $75
Base Case: $125
Bull Case: $155

Bear Probability: 25%
Base Probability: 50%
Bull Probability: 25%
```

Expected price:

```text
Expected Price =
    25% × $75
  + 50% × $125
  + 25% × $155

Expected Price = $120
```

Therefore:

```text
Expected Return = ($120 / $100) - 1
Expected Return = +20%
```

---

# 12. Risk-Adjusted Return

Expected return should be considered alongside downside risk.

```text
Risk-Adjusted Return =
    Expected Return / Downside Risk
```

Example:

| | Stock A | Stock B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Expected Return | 25% | 30% |
| Bear Case | -10% | -40% |
| Risk-Adjusted Return | 2.50 | 0.75 |

A stock with greater nominal upside should not automatically receive a higher attractiveness assessment if its downside is substantially larger.

---

# 13. Conviction Score

The rating and conviction should be separate.

Example:

```text
Stock A
Rating: BUY
Score: 78
Conviction: 86

Stock B
Rating: BUY
Score: 78
Conviction: 53
```

Both receive the same rating, but the first has substantially stronger evidence supporting the score.

## Conviction Formula

```text
Conviction =
    25% Data Quality
  + 25% Model Agreement
  + 20% Estimate Stability
  + 15% Historical Consistency
  + 15% Forecast Dispersion
```

---

# 14. Hard Risk Overrides

The weighted score should not be allowed to hide catastrophic risks.

## Financial Distress Override

If the company has combinations of:

- Extremely negative FCF
- Severe liquidity problems
- Debt distress
- Interest coverage below 1x
- High probability of restructuring

then:

```text
Maximum Rating = SELL
```

unless the model is explicitly configured for distressed/turnaround investing.

---

## Extreme Valuation Override

If:

```text
Forward P/E > 2.5 × Sector Median
```

and:

```text
Expected EPS Growth < 15%
```

apply a severe valuation penalty.

---

## Earnings Deterioration Override

Strongly penalize combinations of:

- Revenue declining
- EPS declining
- FCF declining
- Forward estimates declining

# 15. Sector-Specific Models

The universal model should be supplemented with sector-specific factors.

Do not use identical financial metrics for every industry.

## Banks

Emphasize:

- ROE
- ROTCE
- Net interest margin
- Credit losses
- CET1
- Loan growth
- Deposit growth
- P/TBV

## SaaS

Emphasize:

- ARR
- Revenue growth
- Gross margin
- FCF margin
- Net revenue retention
- Rule of 40
- CAC
- Customer concentration

## Industrials

Emphasize:

- Backlog
- Book-to-bill
- Operating margin
- FCF
- ROIC
- Capex
- Order growth

## REITs

Emphasize:

- AFFO
- FFO
- NAV
- Occupancy
- Same-store NOI
- Debt maturity
- Interest coverage

## Semiconductors

Emphasize:

- Revenue growth
- Gross margin
- Capex
- Inventory
- Book-to-bill
- FCF
- Market share
- End-market exposure

# 18. Relationship to CANSLIM / Kronos

This system should remain **separate from a CANSLIM/Kronos trading model**.

The systems answer different questions.

### Fundamental Engine

> Is this a good company at this price over the next year?

### CANSLIM Engine

> Is this stock exhibiting characteristics associated with a strong intermediate-term opportunity?

### Portfolio Engine

> Should capital actually be allocated to this stock given the rest of the portfolio and its risk?

A future combined system can therefore be structured as:

```text
Fundamental Score
        ×
Market Regime
        ×
Technical Confirmation
        ↓
Investment Score
```

This preserves the distinction between **business quality, valuation, market behavior, and portfolio construction** rather than forcing all of them into one score.
