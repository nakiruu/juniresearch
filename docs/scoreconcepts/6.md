# The Aggregation & Decision Architecture
### How a fundamental view, an expected-value view, and uncertainty combine into one 5-bucket rating

This is the connective-tissue document. Documents `1.md` through `5.md` argue about *what to measure*. This document is about *how the measurements combine into a single defensible call*. It invents no new factor. It specifies the control flow that turns a moat read, an intrinsic-value range, a cross-sectional composite, a set of gates, and the existing reward/risk numbers into one label on the desk's scale — `STRONG SELL / SELL / HOLD / BUY / STRONG BUY` — plus a conviction that travels beside the label rather than inside it.

The live system it must extend is small and specific:

- `lib/synth/conviction.ts` computes `E` (probability-weighted fair value / price − 1), `D` (bear implied price below price, a positive magnitude), and `R = E / D`. `deriveLabel(c, cfg)` maps `(E, R)` to the five buckets using thresholds in `data/desk/desk.json`. `conservativeNotch(label)` returns the one label an author may substitute — always a step toward HOLD, never away from it.
- `lib/synth/merge.ts` computes the conviction object and the derived label; `lib/synth/validate-judgment.ts` enforces that the author's label equals the derived label or its conservative notch, and enforces the 15% bear floor.
- `lib/report.schema.ts` already persists an optional `rating.conviction = { expectedUpside, bearDownside, rewardRisk, derivedLabel }`, and the re-rate batch added a third `RatingBlock` row to show it.

The charter of this document is to make that pipeline deeper without discarding it. The E/R rule stays the valuation spine. Everything else in this document constrains, corroborates, or annotates it.

---

## 0. The shape of the problem

A rating is a decision, not a measurement. The distinction matters because the two existing "fix" documents — `docs/scoreconcepts/1.md` and `docs/scoreconcepts/2.md` — both answer the *measurement* question well (they enumerate the right factors) and then answer the *decision* question with the same tool: a linear weighted sum of 0–100 (or 0–10) sub-scores, mapped to buckets by fixed cutoffs. `1.md` does this at §9 (`Score = Σ Factor × Weight`) and §16; `2.md` does it at §3 (`S = 0.20F + 0.20V + 0.15P + …`) and §5.

A weighted sum is a *compensatory* aggregator: it assumes every input trades off against every other at a fixed exchange rate. That assumption is wrong for equity ratings in ways that are not cosmetic. Sections 1 below is the rigorous version of why. Sections 2–7 are the replacement.

The replacement is a **layered, mostly non-compensatory pipeline** with an explicit control flow, in which the valuation/expected-value engine you already run (`conviction.ts`) proposes a label and the other layers may veto, cap, or annotate it, but rarely improve it.

## 1. Why a linear weighted scorecard is shallow

### 1.1 Compensatory aggregation lets a strong pillar hide a fatal one

In `Score = Σ wᵢ · sᵢ`, a high `sᵢ` on one pillar can offset an arbitrarily low `sⱼ` on another. The math has no concept of "fatal." A company with a going-concern qualification and a covenant breach can still clear 70/100 if its growth and moat sub-scores are high enough, because those points are fungible with the balance-sheet points.

`1.md` recognizes the problem and bolts on §14 "Hard Risk Overrides" and §13's separate conviction; `2.md` bolts on §4 "Hard Vetoes / Caps." That is the correct instinct — but it is an admission that the weighted sum is the wrong *core*. Once you accept that distress must *cap* the rating regardless of other strength, you have accepted that the aggregation is not compensatory. The vetoes are then patches on top of an architecture whose default behavior contradicts them. The right move is to invert the relationship: make the gates the *frame* and the score the *content inside the frame*, not the reverse.

The economic reason compensation fails here: the pillars are not substitutes. Solvency is a precondition for the equity to be worth analyzing at all; it does not "trade off" against gross margin. A 95th-percentile moat does not make a 1.1x interest-coverage ratio safe. Multiplying them and adding is a category error.

### 1.2 Hand-set weights are arbitrary and regime-/sector-blind

`1.md` §9 sets Valuation and Growth at 20% each, Quality and Moat at 15%, and so on. `2.md` §3 sets FCF and Valuation at 20% each. Neither document can justify *why* 20% rather than 15% or 25% — because there is no justification. The weights are priors dressed as parameters.

Worse, a single fixed weight vector is regime- and sector-blind:

- **Regime.** In a disinflationary risk-on regime the market pays for growth; in a credit-stress regime it pays for balance-sheet safety. A static 20/20/15 vector cannot express that the *same* company deserves a different weighting of the same facts across regimes.
- **Sector.** `1.md` §15 concedes this and proposes sector-specific factor *lists*, but keeps the top-level weighting scheme. A bank's rating is dominated by capital adequacy and credit; a pre-profit miner's by balance-sheet runway and commodity price; a payments network's by take-rate durability and regulatory overhang. These are not "re-weightings" of one universal vector — they are *different decision structures*. The desk's own history bears this out: the BAC and JPM runs found industrial EBITDA/FCF/leverage cells "meaningless" for a bank; UEC needed a distress-first read; V needed a moat-first read. A single weight vector applied to all of them is a blunt instrument.

Fixed weights also invite silent overfitting. Whoever picks 20/20/15 is implicitly fitting to the handful of names in their head. There is no out-of-sample discipline on a number that was never estimated from data.

### 1.3 Absolute-threshold cutoffs are discontinuous cliffs

Both documents score metrics by bucketed thresholds — `2.md` §2 gives `FCF Margin >25% → 10`, `10–25% → 7`, `0–10% → 4`. A company at 25.0% margin scores 10; at 24.9% it scores 7. A 0.1% move in an input flips a sub-score by 30% of its range, and the same discontinuity recurs at every band edge of every metric. The final label inherits a lattice of cliffs. Two companies that are economically indistinguishable can land in different buckets because one sits a rounding error on the wrong side of a line.

The desk's existing E/R rule has the same latent problem — `deriveLabel` in `conviction.ts` is a cascade of `>=` tests against `desk.json` thresholds (BUY requires `E ≥ 0.10` **and** `R ≥ 0.50`). An expected upside of 9.99% is HOLD; 10.01% is a BUY candidate. Section 4.3 proposes the fix (a soft border zone that forces a conservative notch instead of letting the call flip), and it must apply to the E/R edges too, not only to the fundamental sub-scores.

### 1.4 Correlated pillars double-count the same signal

A weighted sum treats its inputs as independent contributions. They are not. In `1.md`, "Business Quality" (ROIC, margins), "Competitive Moat" (cost advantage, switching costs), and "Financial Health" (FCF/debt) are three pillars carrying ~40% of the weight between them — and they are strongly collinear. High ROIC *is* largely the financial signature of a moat; strong FCF/debt *is* largely a consequence of high-margin durability. A genuinely wide-moat compounder scores high on all three, so its single underlying advantage is counted roughly three times; a capital-intensive commodity business is penalized three times for one weakness. The effective weight on "durable profitability" is far higher than the nominal 15% label suggests, and nobody set it deliberately. This is the classic multicollinearity failure of additive multi-criteria models: the *stated* weights are not the *realized* weights once the inputs move together.

Valuation and growth are anti-correlated in a different direction — the market prices expected growth into the multiple, so a raw Valuation sub-score and a raw Growth sub-score partly cancel a signal that should be read *jointly* (growth relative to what is priced), not summed.

### 1.5 Point estimates discard confidence

`sᵢ = 82` and `sᵢ = 82` aggregate identically whether the 82 rests on five years of aligned statements and tight analyst dispersion, or on one post-IPO quarter and a 5x spread of price targets. The weighted sum has no channel for confidence. `1.md` §13 sees this and defines a separate Conviction score — correct — but then never lets conviction *act*: it is reported alongside the rating, not allowed to change it. A confidence number that cannot move the decision is decoration.

Uncertainty should do real work: it should widen or narrow the final call (Section 4). The desk's `conservativeNotch` already encodes the germ of this idea — "you may sit one notch toward HOLD" — but leaves it to author discretion. Section 4.4 makes it a function of measured uncertainty.

### 1.6 A single number destroys the "why"

Collapsing everything to 82/100 discards the structure that produced it. Two 82s can mean "cheap, average business, low expectations" and "expensive, exceptional business, high expectations" — opposite theses, identical output. The desk's house style (`desk.json` `styleRules`) demands the opposite: every claim rests on a named figure, and the bear case is a real scenario. A rating architecture that cannot say *why* it is BUY-not-STRONG-BUY, or *which layer* blocked the upgrade, cannot support that prose. The architecture below is designed so that every label carries a machine-readable reason string naming the binding constraint.

### 1.7 What the E/R rule already gets right — and where it is still shallow

`conviction.ts` is better than either scorecard on several of these axes, and the architecture keeps it:

- It is **not a weighted sum of proxies** — it is a probability-weighted valuation, so it aggregates in return space, which is the space the rating lives in.
- `R = E / D` is a **crude loss-asymmetry**: it already refuses to reward upside that is paid for with a deep bear. That is the §1.1 non-compensation instinct, applied to reward vs. risk.
- The `bearFloor` (15%) forces the bear to be a real scenario, blocking the `2.md` §4 critique that a wide unweighted bear can be gamed.

Where it is still shallow, and what this document adds:

1. **It is valuation-only.** E and R say nothing about whether the business is durable, whether the balance sheet survives a shock, or whether earnings are real. A richly-scenario'd fraud can print a clean R. Gates and quality (Sections 2–3) fix this.
2. **E is gameable from the top.** An author who wants BUY can widen the bull target; the `bearFloor` disciplines the bear but nothing disciplines the *bull* or the *base* against an outside intrinsic-value anchor. `4.md` supplies that anchor; Section 3.2 wires it in.
3. **It has cliffs** (§1.3) and **no confidence channel** (§1.5). Sections 4.2–4.3 add both.
4. **An extreme call rests on one view.** STRONG BUY today needs only `E ≥ 20%` and `R ≥ 1.0`. That is a lot of conviction resting on one probability-weighted number. Section 2.4 requires corroboration before any extreme.

## 2. The architecture: a layered, mostly non-compensatory pipeline

### 2.1 Design principles

1. **Lexicographic before compensatory.** Screen on preconditions first (does the equity survive, are the numbers trustworthy). Only inside the survivors do we weigh reward against quality. This is conjunctive screening (all gates must pass) followed by a bounded compensatory step, the standard non-compensatory MCDA pattern.
2. **The valuation engine proposes; other layers dispose.** `conviction.ts` produces the *proposed* label. Every other layer can **downgrade or cap** it and can **lower conviction**; only corroboration among independent layers can *unlock* an extreme. No single fundamental sub-score can *raise* a label on its own. This preserves the desk's existing "never more aggressive than derived" invariant and extends it.
3. **Confidence is a separate axis.** The label answers "which direction and how far"; conviction answers "how much would we stake on it." They are different outputs and are computed by different machinery (Section 4).
4. **Every decision carries its binding reason.** The output is `{ label, conviction, reason }` where `reason` names the layer and rule that set the outcome.

### 2.2 The five layers

```text
   FactPack + sibling-doc outputs (3.md moat, 4.md intrinsic value, 5.md composite+gates)
                                    │
   ┌───────────────────────────────▼───────────────────────────────┐
   │  L0  DISQUALIFYING GATES        (conjunctive; cap the rating)   │
   │      distress · going-concern · earnings-quality / manipulation │
   └───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                                    │  passes → ceiling set
   ┌───────────────────────────────▼───────────────────────────────┐
   │  L1  QUALITY / DURABILITY       (moat width + trend, ROIC-WACC) │
   │      → a modulator in {suppress-extreme, neutral, permit-extreme}│
   └───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
   ┌───────────────────────────────▼───────────────────────────────┐
   │  L2  VALUATION / EXPECTED VALUE (conviction.ts: E, R)           │
   │      disciplined by 4.md intrinsic-value margin of safety (MoS) │
   │      → PROPOSED label = deriveLabel(E,R) with a soft border zone │
   └───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
   ┌───────────────────────────────▼───────────────────────────────┐
   │  L3  CORROBORATION              (do the independent views agree?)│
   │      extreme allowed only if E/R ∧ quality ∧ MoS all agree      │
   │      value/quality disagreement → force toward HOLD             │
   └───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
   ┌───────────────────────────────▼───────────────────────────────┐
   │  L4  UNCERTAINTY / CONVICTION   (dispersion · disagreement ·    │
   │      data completeness) → conviction score + conservative notch │
   └───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
                          { label, conviction, reason }
```

The layers are ordered so that the cheapest, most decisive constraints run first. A failed L0 gate short-circuits the rest.

### 2.3 Control flow (statechart in text)

```text
START
 │
 ├─ L0 GATES ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 │   if hardDistress  (going-concern OR covenant breach OR interest cov <1x
 │                     with negative FCF):        ceiling = SELL   flag = "distress"
 │   elif softDistress (FCF<0 AND netDebt/EBITDA>3 ;  or  Altman-Z distress
 │                     zone ; or manipulation/earnings-quality gate from 5.md):
 │                                                ceiling = HOLD   flag = "gate"
 │   else:                                        ceiling = STRONG BUY
 │
 ├─ L2 PROPOSE (run before L1/L3 use it) ───────────────────────────────────
 │   c = computeConviction(scenarios, price)           // existing
 │   proposed = deriveLabel(c, desk.rating)             // existing
 │   proposed = applyBorderZone(proposed, c, desk.rating)   // §4.3, new
 │
 ├─ L1 QUALITY MODULATOR ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
 │   q = qualityFrom(3.md moat, ROIC−WACC)   // WIDE&improving | adequate | thin
 │   extremeUnlockedByQuality = (q == "WIDE-durable")
 │
 ├─ L4 MoS (from 4.md) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 │   mos = (intrinsicMid − price)/price       // margin of safety, signed
 │
 ├─ L3 CORROBORATION ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 │   label = min(proposed, ceiling)           // gates can only downgrade
 │   if label ∈ {STRONG BUY, STRONG SELL}:
 │        agree = extremeUnlockedByQuality
 │              AND sign(mos)  == sign(E)      // intrinsic value agrees
 │              AND cross-sectional composite (5.md) in same tail
 │        if not agree:  label = oneNotchToward(HOLD, label)   // cap the extreme
 │   if disagree(valuation, quality):          // e.g. cheap-but-eroding, or dear-but-great
 │        label = pull toward HOLD by one notch; record "value/quality split"
 │
 ├─ L4 CONVICTION ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 │   conv = convictionScore(dispersion, layerDisagreement, dataCompleteness)  // §4.2
 │   if conv < desk.rating.conviction.lowThreshold:
 │        label = oneNotchToward(HOLD, label)   // mandated conservative notch (§4.4)
 │
 └─ EMIT { label, conviction: conv, reason }
```

`min(proposed, ceiling)` uses the natural ordering `STRONG SELL < SELL < HOLD < BUY < STRONG BUY`; "downgrade / toward HOLD" moves along it in the HOLD direction. This is the same lattice `conservativeNotch` already walks.

### 2.4 The corroboration truth table

Corroboration is where the architecture stops being a weighted sum and becomes a decision. For the two extreme labels, three independent readings must agree; otherwise the extreme is capped one notch inward. The three readings are deliberately drawn from *different* evidence so their agreement is informative (they are not the collinear pillars of §1.4):

- **V** = the E/R proposal from `conviction.ts` (scenario/valuation view).
- **Q** = quality/durability from `3.md` (moat width + trend, ROIC−WACC).
- **M** = intrinsic-value margin of safety from `4.md` (a DCF/reverse-DCF anchor independent of the author's scenarios).

| V (E/R) | Q (moat) | M (intrinsic MoS) | Label emitted | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STRONG BUY | WIDE-durable | MoS > +cushion | **STRONG BUY** | all three agree — extreme unlocked |
| STRONG BUY | adequate | MoS ≈ 0 | **BUY** | quality/MoS don't corroborate the extreme → cap one notch |
| STRONG BUY | thin/eroding | MoS < 0 | **HOLD/BUY** | valuation optimism unsupported by value or quality → cap toward HOLD |
| BUY | WIDE-durable | MoS > 0 | **BUY** | corroborated non-extreme; conviction high |
| BUY | thin | MoS < 0 | **HOLD** (via L4 notch) | cheap on scenarios only; value & quality dissent |
| HOLD | WIDE-durable | MoS > 0 | **HOLD → BUY only if V lifts** | quality alone cannot lift a HOLD (principle 2) |
| SELL | any | MoS < 0 | **SELL** | downside corroborated |
| STRONG SELL | any | MoS ≪ 0 & distress flag | **STRONG SELL** | extreme-down unlocked by gate + value |
| STRONG SELL | WIDE-durable | MoS > 0 | **SELL** | a great cheap business should not be a STRONG SELL on scenarios alone |

The asymmetry is intentional and matches the desk's existing conservatism: **corroboration is required to reach an extreme, and disagreement always resolves toward HOLD.** Quality can *permit* an extreme (by corroborating) and can *block* one (by dissenting); it can never *manufacture* one. That is principle 2 made concrete, and it directly answers the owner's note that "risk/reward is an improvement but not a total win" — the win it lacks is exactly this cross-view agreement requirement.

### 2.5 Why non-compensatory, in one contrast

Take a company at `E = +24%`, `R = 1.1` (clears STRONG BUY on E/R) but with a 5% shareholder-dilution overhang, an interest-coverage of 1.4x on negative FCF, and a fresh restatement.

- **Weighted sum (`1.md`/`2.md`):** growth and valuation sub-scores are high; the distress veto (`2.md` §4) *might* cap it at Hold if the author remembered to encode the exact `FCF<0 AND ND/EBITDA>3` trigger — but the manipulation signal has no cell, so it silently contributes nothing.
- **This architecture:** L0's earnings-quality gate (from `5.md`) fires on the restatement → ceiling = HOLD before E/R is even consulted; the soft-distress gate independently caps at HOLD; corroboration never runs because the extreme is already unreachable. The reason string reads `"gate: earnings-quality (restatement) capped at HOLD"`. The label is HOLD *for a stated reason*, not because points happened to net out below a cutoff.

## 3. Reconciling the fundamental view with the conviction (E, R)

This is the crux, so it is specified concretely.

### 3.1 The one-way rule

> **The E/R rule sets the ceiling of optimism. Fundamentals and uncertainty can only lower or corroborate it.**

Formally, let `proposed = deriveLabel(c, desk.rating)`. The emitted label satisfies, at every step:

```
emitted ≤ proposed        (never more bullish than the E/R proposal — the existing invariant)
emitted ≤ ceiling(L0)      (gates cap it)
emitted = proposed only if corroborated (L3) and confident (L4); else toward HOLD
```

The single exception where a layer may push *below* what E/R alone implies is **downgrade**: a gate or a value/quality split can move the label toward (and past) HOLD into SELL territory even if E/R said HOLD. This is deliberate — a solvency problem should be able to produce a SELL that a merely-flat expected value would not. It never moves *away* from HOLD on the bullish side without corroboration.

### 3.2 The interaction rules, explicitly

1. **Gate → cap.** L0 sets `ceiling ∈ {SELL, HOLD, STRONG BUY}`. `emitted = min(proposed, ceiling)`. A distress gate can turn an E/R BUY into a HOLD or SELL. Nothing turns a gated name into a BUY.
2. **Intrinsic value → discipline E.** `4.md` returns an intrinsic-value range. Two uses:
   - **Sanity-bound the scenarios.** If the author's probability-weighted fair value sits above `4.md`'s high intrinsic estimate, flag it (`"E exceeds intrinsic ceiling"`). This disciplines the *bull/base* the way `bearFloor` disciplines the bear today — closing the §1.7(2) gap. Implemented as a new check in `validate-judgment.ts`, severity Important.
   - **Corroborate the extreme.** MoS sign must match E's sign for a STRONG call (table §2.4).
3. **Value/quality disagreement → HOLD pull.** If E/R is bullish but quality is thin, or E/R is bearish but the business is wide-moat and cheap on intrinsic value, force one notch toward HOLD and lower conviction. Disagreement is information: it says the market and the fundamentals are telling different stories, and the desk's response is to not take an extreme position on a contested read.
4. **STRONG BUY unlock.** Requires *all* of: `E ≥ desk.rating.strongBuy.minUpside`, `R ≥ desk.rating.strongBuy.minRewardRisk` (existing), **and** quality = WIDE-durable (`3.md`), **and** MoS > `desk.rating.strongBuy.minMarginOfSafety` (new), **and** the `5.md` composite in the top cross-sectional band. STRONG SELL is the mirror, with the distress gate standing in for the moat condition.
5. **Uncertainty → mandated notch.** Low conviction (Section 4) converts the *optional* `conservativeNotch` into a *required* one.

### 3.3 Extending `conviction.ts`

The existing functions are untouched; the architecture wraps them. New code lives beside them so the E/R spine stays independently testable.

```ts
// conviction.ts — existing, unchanged:
//   computeConviction(scenarios, price) -> { expectedUpside, bearDownside, rewardRisk }
//   deriveLabel(c, cfg) -> RatingLabel
//   conservativeNotch(label) -> RatingLabel | null

// --- new: the layers as pure functions over already-computed inputs ---

export type Ceiling = "SELL" | "HOLD" | "STRONG BUY";
export interface GateResult { ceiling: Ceiling; flags: string[] }        // from 5.md + FactPack
export type Quality = "WIDE-durable" | "adequate" | "thin";               // from 3.md
export interface ValueAnchor { mos: number; withinBand: boolean }          // from 4.md

export interface Decision { label: RatingLabel; conviction: number; reason: string }

const ORDER: RatingLabel[] = ["STRONG SELL", "SELL", "HOLD", "BUY", "STRONG BUY"];
const idx = (l: RatingLabel) => ORDER.indexOf(l);
const towardHold = (l: RatingLabel) =>
  l === "HOLD" ? l : idx(l) > idx("HOLD") ? ORDER[idx(l) - 1] : ORDER[idx(l) + 1];
const capAt = (l: RatingLabel, ceil: RatingLabel) => (idx(l) <= idx(ceil) ? l : ceil);

export function decide(
  c: Conviction, cfg: DeskRating,
  gate: GateResult, q: Quality, v: ValueAnchor, conv: ConvictionScore,
): Decision {
  let label = applyBorderZone(deriveLabel(c, cfg), c, cfg);   // §4.3
  let reason = `E/R proposed ${label}`;

  // L0 gates cap
  label = capAt(label, gate.ceiling);
  if (label !== reasonLabel(reason)) reason = `gate(${gate.flags.join(",")}) capped ${label}`;

  // L3 corroboration for extremes
  if (label === "STRONG BUY" || label === "STRONG SELL") {
    const agree = label === "STRONG BUY"
      ? q === "WIDE-durable" && v.mos > (cfg.strongBuy.minMarginOfSafety ?? 0) && conv.tailAgrees
      : gate.flags.includes("distress") && v.mos < 0 && conv.tailAgrees;
    if (!agree) { label = towardHold(label); reason = `extreme not corroborated → ${label}`; }
  }

  // value/quality split
  if (valueQualitySplit(c, q, v)) { label = towardHold(label); reason = `value/quality split → ${label}`; }

  // L4 uncertainty mandates a notch
  if (conv.score < cfg.conviction.lowThreshold) {
    const n = towardHold(label);
    if (n !== label) { label = n; reason = `low conviction (${conv.score}) → ${label}`; }
  }
  return { label, conviction: conv.score, reason };
}
```

`decide` is the successor to the two lines in `merge.ts` (§0). `merge.ts` already calls `computeConviction` + `deriveLabel`; it would instead call `decide` and persist the whole `Decision`. `validate-judgment.ts` currently checks `label ∈ {derived, conservativeNotch(derived)}`; it would check `label === decide(...).label` (or the one conservative notch of it, keeping author latitude), and add the intrinsic-ceiling check of §3.2(2).

### 3.4 New `desk.json` configuration

Everything the architecture reads that is not a fact is desk config, so it lives in `data/desk/desk.json` under `rating`, beside the existing thresholds — no code constants (the schema in `lib/synth/desk.schema.ts` gains matching optional fields with defaults, so old reports still parse):

```jsonc
"rating": {
  "bearFloor": 0.15,
  "strongBuy": { "minUpside": 0.2, "minRewardRisk": 1.0, "minMarginOfSafety": 0.10 },
  "buy":       { "minUpside": 0.1, "minRewardRisk": 0.5 },
  "sell":      { "maxUpside": -0.05 },
  "strongSell":{ "maxUpside": -0.2 },
  "borderZone": 0.02,               // ±2% of a band edge → force conservative notch (§4.3)
  "gates": {
    "hardInterestCoverage": 1.0,    // <1x with negative FCF → ceiling SELL
    "softLeverage": 3.0,            // FCF<0 AND netDebt/EBITDA>this → ceiling HOLD
    "dilutionWatch": 0.05           // >5% share growth → conviction penalty + risk flag
  },
  "conviction": {
    "lowThreshold": 45,             // below → mandated conservative notch
    "weights": { "dispersion": 0.4, "disagreement": 0.35, "completeness": 0.25 }
  }
}
```

Keeping these in `desk.json` means the desk tunes the decision policy the way it already tunes lint and rating thresholds, and the values are visible to reviewers rather than buried in code.

## 4. Uncertainty and conviction as a first-class output

The rating says *where*; conviction says *how sure*. `report.schema.ts` already reserves `rating.conviction` — today it holds `{ expectedUpside, bearDownside, rewardRisk, derivedLabel }`, i.e. the E/R mechanics and the derived label, not a confidence measure. The proposal is to *add* a confidence score and its drivers to that same object, so the persisted shape becomes:

```ts
conviction: {
  expectedUpside, bearDownside, rewardRisk, derivedLabel,   // existing
  score,          // 0–100 confidence (new)
  tier,           // "high" | "moderate" | "low"  (new)
  drivers,        // { dispersion, disagreement, completeness } (new)
  reason,         // binding-constraint string from decide() (new)
}
```

The third `RatingBlock` row added by the re-rate batch already renders `derivedLabel`; it gains a `score`/`tier` chip beside it. No page rebuild is required for old reports because the field is optional.

### 4.1 Three sources of uncertainty

1. **Analyst-target dispersion.** The spread of `analysts.highTarget` / `lowTarget` around the median, normalized by price. A tight band is agreement; a 4–5x spread is not. This is a market proxy for how contested the name is.
2. **Cross-layer disagreement.** The variance among the three corroboration readings V/Q/M (§2.4). When E/R, quality, and intrinsic value point different directions, the desk's own analysis is internally contested — the strongest confidence discount, because it is not borrowed from the crowd.
3. **Data completeness.** How much of the FactPack the layers could actually use: aligned fiscal-year count, whether peers carry live multiples, whether segments/geo are populated, whether TTM EPS could be summed. The desk has hit every one of these — young issuers with three aligned years (CRWV), null peer multiples (AMD's peers below), multi-class EPS that companyfacts omits (V). Thin data should lower confidence, not silently pass through as if certain.

### 4.2 Constructing the conviction score

```text
dispersionPenalty   = clamp( (highTarget − lowTarget) / medianTarget , 0..1 )
disagreementPenalty = spread({ rank(V), rank(Q), rank(M) }) / maxSpread      # 0..1
incompleteness      = 1 − (fieldsUsable / fieldsExpected)                    # 0..1

conviction = 100 · (1 −  w_d·dispersionPenalty
                       −  w_g·disagreementPenalty
                       −  w_c·incompleteness )
             with weights from desk.rating.conviction.weights
```

This is intentionally a *penalty* model, not another compensatory sum of virtues: confidence starts at 100 and each source of doubt subtracts. It cannot be inflated by piling on bullish factors — the §1.5 failure of a decorative conviction score. The weights live in `desk.json` (§3.4).

Note the deliberate separation from the *rating*: a stock can be a high-conviction HOLD (everything agrees it is fairly valued) or a low-conviction BUY (cheap on scenarios but contested and thinly-covered). The re-rate batch's own framing — label and conviction persisted side by side — is exactly this.

### 4.3 Loss-asymmetric bucket mapping instead of fixed cutoffs

Two refinements replace the hard cutoffs of §1.3 without discarding the desk's thresholds:

**(a) Certainty-equivalent return.** Rather than mapping on the point estimate `E`, map on a downside-penalized certainty equivalent:

```
CE = E − λ · D                 # λ from desk config; D is the bear magnitude conviction.ts already returns
```

Because `D` is already computed and `R = E/D`, this is a smooth monotone transform of the same surface the desk trusts: a name with a deep bear is pushed down *continuously* as the bear deepens, instead of only tripping the discrete `R ≥ 0.5` gate. `deriveLabel` still applies the desk's upside thresholds, but to `CE` on the bullish side, so a wide bear no longer has to reach an exact `R` line to matter. This is expected-utility reasoning (a concave, loss-averse utility) reduced to one tunable, keeping the code auditable.

**(b) Border zone.** For the residual discreteness at each band edge, `applyBorderZone` widens each threshold into a no-man's-land of `±desk.rating.borderZone`. If `(E, R, CE)` lands inside a border zone, the label does **not** flip to the more aggressive side; it takes the conservative side and records `"border zone"`. A 0.1% move can no longer change a *published* call — it can only change it once the metric clears the edge by the full border width. This generalizes the spirit of `bearFloor` (a buffer that forces the safe interpretation near a boundary) to every rating edge.

### 4.4 Generalizing the conservative-notch rule

Today `conservativeNotch` is a *permission*: the author may sit one notch toward HOLD. Two generalizations, both continuous with the existing rule:

- **Low conviction makes the notch mandatory.** If `conviction < lowThreshold`, the emitted label is moved one notch toward HOLD automatically (L4 in §2.3). "One notch more conservative when uncertain" is thus the same operation the desk already trusts, now *triggered by a measured quantity* instead of author judgment. Author discretion remains for the *permitted* notch on top.
- **Severe disagreement can mandate two notches (collapse to HOLD).** When all three uncertainty sources are high at once, the safe call is HOLD regardless of E/R — the architecture simply refuses to take a side it cannot support. This is bounded (§7.1 discusses the risk of over-collapsing).

The invariant that survives all of this: **the machinery only ever moves the label toward HOLD or corroborates it outward; it never moves it further from HOLD than the E/R proposal permits.** That is the existing `conviction.ts` contract, preserved.

## 5. Worked example — AMD (FY2026 Q2 FactPack)

Using `data/facts/AMD/0000002488-26-000123.json` (price $504.20 as of 2026-09-15). This walks one company through all five layers and contrasts the result with (a) a plain weighted sum and (b) the pure E/R rule.

**Scenario set (illustrative, consistent with the desk's stored AMD HOLD, `R ≈ 0.37`):**

| Scenario | Implied price | Prob | Weighted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | $355 | 0.30 | $106.50 |
| Base | $565 | 0.45 | $254.25 |
| Bull | $790 | 0.25 | $197.50 |
| **Fair value** | | | **$558.25** |

- `E` = 558.25 / 504.20 − 1 = **+10.7%**
- `D` = (504.20 − 355) / 504.20 = **29.6%** (bear sits well below the 15% `bearFloor` — a real scenario)
- `R` = 10.7 / 29.6 = **0.36**

**L0 — Gates.** Net debt is negative (−$1,067M, net cash); interest coverage 44.1x; current ratio 2.61; FY25 FCF +$6,735M; TTM net margin 15.6%. No distress. Earnings-quality: clean. One flag worth carrying — the OpenAI + Meta warrants total 320M shares at $0.01 exercise against 1,630.6M outstanding (~20% potential dilution). That is not a solvency gate but it is a `dilutionWatch` flag → conviction penalty and a named risk, not a cap. **Ceiling = STRONG BUY (no cap).**

**L2 — Valuation / E,R.** `deriveLabel`: `E = 10.7%` clears BUY's `minUpside` (0.10), but `R = 0.36` **fails** BUY's `minRewardRisk` (0.50). Not STRONG BUY (needs `E ≥ 20%`, `R ≥ 1.0`). Not SELL (`E` is positive). **Proposed = HOLD.** The certainty-equivalent refinement makes this robust: `CE = 10.7% − λ·29.6%` is comfortably sub-BUY for any reasonable λ, so this is not a border-zone call — it is a clear HOLD.

**L1 — Quality.** AMD is a strong business: FY25 revenue $34.6B (+34%), Data Center now ~58% of revenue and growing >100% YoY, EPYC share gains, a credible MI400/Helios roadmap. Moat (from `3.md`) is best read as **adequate/narrowing-to-wide** in data-center compute — real but contested by NVIDIA and by Arm ecosystems the 10-Q's own risk factors name. Not "WIDE-durable" in the sense the extreme unlock requires.

**L4 anchor — Intrinsic value / MoS.** Valuation is the whole problem: TTM P/E **128x**, EV/EBITDA **76.6x**, FCF yield **1.02%**. Forward multiples compress (FY26E EPS $7.62 → 66x; FY27E EPS $15.84 → 32x) but only on estimates that already embed the AI ramp. A DCF/reverse-DCF anchor from `4.md` at these multiples yields **MoS ≈ 0 or negative** at the mid — the price already pays for years of the growth. `mos` does not agree in sign with a *strong* upside case.

**L3 — Corroboration.** Proposed is HOLD, not an extreme, so the STRONG-BUY table row is moot. But note the value/quality split: quality is bullish, valuation/MoS is neutral-to-bearish. That split is exactly what should keep AMD at HOLD rather than letting the strong business narrative pull it up. No upward movement.

**L4 — Conviction.**
- Dispersion: target low $260, high $1250, median $625 → `(1250−260)/625 = 1.58`, clamped to 1.0 — **maximal dispersion penalty.** The Street does not agree on this name.
- Disagreement: V=HOLD, Q=bullish, M=neutral/bearish → moderate-to-high split.
- Completeness: all four peers (`AMAT, ARM, ASML, KLAC`) carry **null** `pe/ps/evToEbitda` → the cross-sectional composite (`5.md`) cannot be grounded; a completeness penalty.
- Result: **low conviction** (well under the `lowThreshold`). Because the label is already HOLD, the mandated notch has nowhere further to move it toward — HOLD absorbs it. Conviction is emitted as **low**.

**Emitted:** `{ label: "HOLD", conviction: low, reason: "value/quality split; low conviction (target dispersion 1.58, peer multiples missing)" }`.

**Contrast:**

- **Versus a plain weighted sum (`1.md`/`2.md`).** AMD's growth, quality, moat, and financial-health sub-scores are all high — `1.md`'s own worked example scores a comparable AI name in the 90s across those pillars. A 20/20/15 weighting lands AMD at BUY or STRONG BUY, with valuation (one 20% pillar at a low sub-score) unable to outvote four strong pillars — the §1.1 and §1.4 failures in one name (durable-profitability counted three times, valuation once). The architecture instead lets the valuation/E-R spine set the ceiling at HOLD and uses quality only to *check* the extreme, never to manufacture a BUY.
- **Versus pure E/R.** Here they agree on the *label* (HOLD), which is the honest outcome — but the architecture adds two things E/R alone cannot: an explicit **low-conviction tag** driven by the 4.8x target spread and the missing peer data, and a **named reason**. And it closes the gameability gap: had the author drawn a bull-heavy scenario set to force `E = +22%`, `R = 1.1` (pure E/R would then print STRONG BUY), the corroboration layer would still **cap at BUY** — quality is not WIDE-durable and MoS is not positive at 66–128x earnings — with reason `"extreme not corroborated (MoS<0, moat adequate)"`. That cap is precisely the "risk/reward is not a total win" gap the owner identified.

## 6. How the other concept documents plug in

This document is the framework; `3.md`, `4.md`, and `5.md` are the layers it consumes. Each must expose a small, typed interface so `decide()` (§3.3) never re-derives their internals.

### 6.1 `3.md` — Moat / durability → the quality layer and a modulator

**Consumes:** segments, margins, ROIC vs. WACC, competitive risk-factor language, moat factors.
**Must output:**

```ts
interface MoatRead {
  width: "WIDE" | "NARROW" | "NONE";        // aligns with judgment.schema businessMoat.moatRating
  trend: "improving" | "stable" | "eroding";
  roicSpread: number | null;                 // ROIC − WACC, the quantitative check
}
```

The architecture maps `(width, trend, roicSpread)` to `Quality ∈ {WIDE-durable, adequate, thin}`. Only `WIDE + (improving|stable) + roicSpread>0` unlocks a STRONG BUY (§2.4). `eroding` is a value/quality-split trigger (§3.2.3). This is the layer's dual role: a **gate on extremes** and a **modulator toward HOLD**.

### 6.2 `4.md` — Intrinsic value & implied expectations → disciplines E and the valuation layer

**Consumes:** statements, TTM, estimates, price; a DCF and a reverse-DCF.
**Must output:**

```ts
interface ValueRead {
  intrinsicLow: number; intrinsicMid: number; intrinsicHigh: number;  // per-share
  impliedGrowth: number;      // growth the current price already requires (reverse-DCF)
  mos: number;                // (intrinsicMid − price)/price, signed
}
```

Two hooks: `mos` feeds corroboration (§2.4) and the STRONG-BUY unlock; `intrinsicHigh` bounds the author's fair value in `validate-judgment.ts` (§3.2.2), disciplining the *bull/base* the way `bearFloor` disciplines the bear. `impliedGrowth` is the narrative check the desk keeps re-learning (NVDA "cheap on earnings but the price implies a lot"; AMD above) — it belongs in the prose, sourced from this layer.

### 6.3 `5.md` — Cross-sectional composite + earnings-quality / distress gates → L0 and a tail check

**Consumes:** the full FactPack plus a peer/sector cross-section.
**Must output:**

```ts
interface ScreenRead {
  gate: { ceiling: "SELL" | "HOLD" | "STRONG BUY"; flags: string[] };  // distress, manipulation, going-concern
  composite: { percentile: number | null; usableFactors: number };     // cross-sectional rank, 0..1
}
```

`gate` *is* L0 — the conjunctive screen that caps the rating before E/R is consulted. `composite.percentile` is the third tail-agreement input for extremes (§2.4) and, when `null` (as with AMD's null-multiple peers), directly raises the completeness penalty in the conviction score (§4.2). Note the division of labor: `5.md` owns the *distress/earnings-quality* gates; this document owns *how a passed or failed gate changes the label*.

### 6.4 The combined interface

`decide()` takes exactly one struct assembled from the three:

```ts
interface LayerInputs {
  conviction: Conviction;   // conviction.ts (this repo, existing)
  moat: MoatRead;           // 3.md
  value: ValueRead;         // 4.md
  screen: ScreenRead;       // 5.md
}
```

If any sibling layer is unavailable, the architecture degrades gracefully to the current behavior: with `moat`/`value`/`screen` absent, `decide()` reduces to `deriveLabel` + `conservativeNotch` — i.e. exactly today's `conviction.ts`. That makes the rollout incremental and the layers independently testable.

## 7. Limitations, failure modes, and validation

### 7.1 Failure modes

1. **Veto collapse — everything becomes HOLD.** The gravest risk. Stack enough conjunctive gates, disagreement pulls, and mandated notches and the architecture answers HOLD to almost everything — safe and useless. Mitigations: (a) gates must be *rare and severe* (true distress/manipulation), audited for hit-rate — if a gate fires on >~10–15% of names it is miscalibrated; (b) the disagreement pull is *one* notch, not a collapse, except when all three uncertainty sources are simultaneously high; (c) monitor the emitted-label distribution against the E/R-only distribution and alarm if HOLD share inflates beyond a set margin.
2. **Gate overfitting.** Thresholds like `netDebt/EBITDA > 3` or `interestCoverage < 1x` are easy to tune to the last blow-up. Keep them few, economically motivated, and set from a broad panel — not from the current watchlist. Version them in `desk.json` so a change is reviewable and reversible.
3. **Corroboration hides a correct contrarian call.** Requiring V∧Q∧M to agree for an extreme means a genuinely mispriced name where only valuation sees it will be capped at BUY, not STRONG BUY. That is an accepted cost: the desk's posture (`conservativeNotch`, `bearFloor`) is already "give up some extreme calls to avoid unsupported ones." It should be a *conscious* trade, tracked (see 7.2).
4. **Layer inputs are themselves models.** MoS from `4.md` and moat from `3.md` carry their own error. Feeding them into gates risks importing their mistakes. Guard: MoS enters only as a *sign* check and a *soft* discipline, never as a hard numeric gate; a wrong DCF should cost a notch, not a category.
5. **Certainty-equivalent λ and border-zone width are new free parameters.** They can be tuned to produce any desired answer. Fix them from data (7.2), pin them in `desk.json`, and treat a change as a policy change, not a bug fix.

### 7.2 Validation

- **Label stability / no-cliff test.** Perturb each continuous input by ±0.5–1% and confirm published labels do not flip except when a metric clears a border zone by its full width. This is the direct test that §1.3/§4.3 worked.
- **Ablation.** Run E/R-only vs. +gates vs. +corroboration vs. full stack over the ~22 published reports (AMD through LRCX and the re-rate batch). Each layer should change a *small*, *explainable* set of names (AMD's BUY→HOLD flip in the re-rate batch is the template). If a layer moves many names, it is doing too much.
- **Gate hit-rate audit.** Fraction of the universe each gate caps; flag any gate firing too often or never.
- **Reason-string coverage.** Every non-HOLD label must carry a binding reason that a human reviewer accepts. This is the §1.6 "why" test, made checkable, and it plugs into the existing editorial/desk-lint gate.
- **Out-of-sample discipline for weights and thresholds.** Any parameter estimated from history (conviction weights, λ, gate levels) is set on a training window and confirmed to hold on a held-out window, to avoid re-introducing the silent overfitting of hand-set weights (§1.2). Beware overfitting the *gates* specifically — a gate is a step function and a single tuned threshold can memorize the training set.
- **Backtest caveat.** A rating architecture is not a trading strategy; a 12-month-horizon label backtest is thin (few independent observations per name) and survivorship-prone. Treat backtests as a sanity floor (does STRONG SELL correlate with subsequent underperformance at all?), not as an optimization target.

### 7.3 What this does not fix

The architecture combines views; it cannot create signal that the layers do not carry. If `3.md`'s moat read is wrong, `4.md`'s DCF is anchored on bad estimates, or the FactPack is thin (young issuers, foreign filers, banks with unclassified balance sheets — all in the desk's history), the output degrades. The honest response in those cases is a **low-conviction** label, which is exactly what Section 4 is built to emit — the architecture's job is to *say when it does not know*, not to pretend a number.

## 8. Summary

- The two existing scorecards (`1.md`, `2.md`) and, to a lesser degree, the live E/R rule are **compensatory**: strong factors offset fatal ones, weights are arbitrary and static, cutoffs are cliffs, correlated pillars double-count, point estimates hide confidence, and the single number destroys the reasoning.
- The replacement is a **layered, mostly non-compensatory pipeline**: disqualifying **gates** cap the rating, a **quality/durability** read modulates it, the existing **E/R valuation** engine proposes it, **corroboration** among independent views (valuation, moat, intrinsic value) is required before any extreme, and an **uncertainty** layer emits a conviction score and can mandate a conservative notch.
- It **reconciles with `conviction.ts`** by making the E/R proposal the ceiling of optimism: gates and disagreement can only lower or corroborate it, corroboration can unlock an extreme, and nothing makes the label more bullish than E/R allows — the desk's existing "never more aggressive than derived, one notch more conservative permitted" invariant, generalized and made a function of measured uncertainty. New policy lives in `desk.json`, new code wraps (does not replace) `conviction.ts`, and the persisted `rating.conviction` object gains a real confidence score.
- Its **main limitation** is veto collapse — too many gates and pulls can drive everything to HOLD — which is managed by keeping gates rare and severe, capping disagreement pulls at one notch, and auditing the emitted-label distribution and gate hit-rates against the E/R-only baseline.
