# Document 3 — The Economic-Moat & Capital-Returns Durability Engine

## What this document is

Documents `1.md` and `2.md` both try to add a "fundamentals" layer to the rating.
Both do it the same way: a **flat linear scorecard**. In `1.md`, "Competitive Moat"
is 15% of a 0–100 sum, and inside that a subjective set of hand-weighted
sub-scores (Switching Costs 15%, Network Effects 15%, …) that a human would have
to guess. In `2.md`, moat quality never appears at all except indirectly through
ROIC in the "Profitability" pillar. In both, a moat is *one weight among eight*,
set once, absolute, and averaged away by everything else.

This engine takes a different position:

> A moat is not a score. It is the **persistence of excess returns on capital**
> — how far, how steadily, and for how long a company earns above its cost of
> capital — measured from the statements, and its **trend** over time. That
> persistence should not be *added* to a rating. It should **gate and reshape**
> the reward/risk rule the rating already rests on.

Two things follow from that position and separate this document from `1.md`/`2.md`:

1. The output is **not a number on a 0–100 axis**. It is a two-part verdict —
   a **Width** (`None` / `Narrow` / `Wide`) and a **Trend**
   (`Widening` / `Stable` / `Eroding`) — each produced by explicit decision
   rules over an evidence series, in the spirit of Morningstar's economic-moat
   rating and Mauboussin's work on ROIC fade and the competitive-advantage
   period (CAP), not by blending sub-weights.
2. The verdict does not enter a weighted sum. It **wraps `lib/synth/conviction.ts`**:
   it sets the minimum bear depth the scenarios must respect (which moves `R`),
   caps which of the five buckets is reachable, and constrains the fair-value
   multiple the scenarios may defend. `E`, `D`, `R` and `deriveLabel` are left
   intact; the moat decides *how far they are allowed to travel*.

The rest of this document specifies the series, the two verdicts, the modulation,
and works the whole thing through AMD's real FactPack
(`data/facts/AMD/0000002488-26-000123.json`).

Cross-references, not reproduced here: `4.md` (reverse-DCF / market-implied CAP)
consumes the fade rate this engine estimates; `5.md` (multi-factor + distress
gates) is a *cross-sectional* peer-relative layer where this engine is a
*time-series, single-name* layer; `6.md` decides how this modulation composes
with the others.

## 1. Moat as persistence of excess returns

The economic content of a moat is a single, measurable idea: **the company earns
a return on invested capital above the cost of that capital, and competition
cannot compete it away quickly.** Everything qualitative — switching costs,
network effects, scale, brand — is a *cause*. The *evidence* is the ROIC–WACC
spread and how long it survives.

So the engine is built on three series, all derivable from the FactPack's five
fiscal years plus the quote block:

- a **ROIC series** (5 points),
- a **WACC estimate** (one number, with a flagged proxy),
- the **excess-return spread** = ROIC − WACC (5 points),

plus two corroborating series that catch what a single-year spread misses:

- **incremental ROIC** = ΔNOPAT / ΔInvested Capital over the window (the
  economics of *newly deployed* capital, which is what a moat has to defend), and
- **gross-margin level and slope** (a direct pricing-power read that is not
  distorted by capital-structure noise).

### 1.1 The ROIC series from five years of statements

Every input below is a row already present in `statements.income`,
`statements.balance`, or `statements.cashflow`.

```
NOPAT_t          = operatingIncome_t × (1 − t_eff)
InvestedCapital_t = totalDebt_t + totalEquity_t − cashAndInvestments_t
ROIC_t           = NOPAT_t / InvestedCapital_t
```

`operatingIncome`, `totalDebt`, `totalEquity`, `cashAndInvestments` are all
FactPack rows. `t_eff` (effective tax rate) is **not** in the pack and cannot be
reliably backed out of `netIncome / operatingIncome` — non-operating gains, DTA
releases and one-time tax items break that ratio (AMD FY25 net income of
$4,335M actually *exceeds* operating income of $3,694M). So `t_eff` is a
**documented assumption**: default to a sector marginal rate (this engine uses
15% for semiconductors; 21% U.S. statutory is the conservative fallback), and
flag actual tax expense as a wanted FactPack field (see §8). ROIC is reported
with its tax assumption stated so the reader can see the sensitivity.

Invested capital is defined from the financing side (debt + equity − cash)
because those three rows exist; the operating-asset definition
(net working capital + net PP&E + intangibles) would be cleaner but the pack
does not expose the line items. This choice has one important consequence,
handled in §2.

### 1.2 A WACC proxy — and why it is a flagged input

The FactPack contains **no beta, no risk-free rate, no equity-risk premium**, so
a textbook WACC cannot be computed from it. The honest design is a **build-up
with two clearly-flagged macro inputs and a sector beta**, defaulting to a
sector hurdle when even those are absent:

```
wE   = marketCap / (marketCap + totalDebt)          # from quote + balance
wD   = totalDebt / (marketCap + totalDebt)
ke   = rf + β_sector × ERP                            # rf, ERP, β = REQUIRED inputs
kd   = rf + creditSpread(interestCoverage)            # spread from a coverage table
WACC = wE × ke + wD × kd × (1 − t_eff)
```

- `rf` (risk-free) and `ERP` (equity risk premium) are **macro inputs the
  desk must supply** — one pair for all names, e.g. `rf = 4.3%`, `ERP = 4.5%`.
- `β_sector` is a **required new field**; until it exists, fall back to a small
  sector-beta table (semis ≈ 1.7, staples ≈ 0.6, utilities ≈ 0.5, banks ≈ 1.1).
- `creditSpread(interestCoverage)` maps `ttm.interestCoverage` to a spread
  (>15× → 1.0%, 8–15× → 1.5%, 4–8× → 2.5%, <4× → 4.0%+). For net-cash names the
  debt weight is tiny, so this term barely matters.

If none of `rf`/`ERP`/`β` is available, the engine uses a **sector hurdle table**
directly (semis 12%, software 10%, staples 7%, utilities 6.5%, banks — use ROE
vs cost of equity instead, see §8) and marks the WACC as `proxy: sector-default`.
Either way the WACC used is recorded in the report so the spread is auditable.

WACC is deliberately the **one place** the engine reaches outside the FactPack.
That is a feature: it is isolated, flagged, and sensitivity-tested, not smuggled
into a dozen sub-weights.

### 1.3 The excess-return spread

```
Spread_t = ROIC_t − WACC
```

The spread series — its **level**, its **stability**, and its **slope** — is the
raw material for Width and Trend. Nothing else in this engine is a hand-set
weight; the thresholds below are cutoffs on this measured series.

## 2. Two problems a single-year ROIC hides (and how the engine handles them)

A flat scorecard reads one ROIC number and scores it. Two real-world effects make
that dangerous, and both show up in AMD:

**(a) Large stock-funded acquisitions blow up invested capital.** When a company
buys another for stock, equity and goodwill jump in one year, the ROIC
denominator balloons, and reported ROIC collapses even though the *operating*
business is unchanged or better. The engine detects this with a **structural-break
test**:

```
break_t  = |InvestedCapital_t − InvestedCapital_{t−1}| / InvestedCapital_{t−1} > 0.50
```

When a break is flagged, the pre-break years are marked **non-comparable**, Width
and Trend are computed on the post-break window, and **incremental ROIC** (which
measures the capital deployed *since* the break) is weighted more heavily than the
average ROIC (which is dragged by the one-time goodwill slug).

**(b) Non-cash intangible amortization depresses accounting ROIC below cash
returns.** Acquisition intangibles amortize through operating income for years,
so NOPAT-based ROIC understates the cash the business throws off. The engine
carries a **cash cross-check**:

```
FCF-ROIC_t = freeCashFlow_t / InvestedCapital_t
```

When FCF-ROIC materially exceeds NOPAT-ROIC, the gap is reported as
"accounting drag" and the width verdict leans on the cash series. Both problems
are exactly what a 0–100 moat weight cannot see.

## 3. Moat WIDTH — `None` / `Narrow` / `Wide` from evidence

Width is decided by **rules over five evidence tests**, not by averaging them.
Each test reads the series from §1–§2 and returns a category. A combining rule
(closer to a ratings committee than to a spreadsheet) resolves them, and a
conflict-resolution clause handles the acquisition case.

### 3.1 The five evidence tests

| # | Test | Field basis | `Wide` | `Narrow` | `None` |
|---|------|-------------|--------|----------|--------|
| A | **Excess-return level** — median `Spread_t` over comparable window | ROIC − WACC | ≥ +8 pts | 0 to +8 pts | < 0 |
| B | **Excess-return stability** — share of comparable years with `Spread_t > 0`; ROIC coefficient of variation | ROIC series | ≥ 80% positive **and** CoV < 0.35 | 40–80% positive | < 40% positive |
| C | **Incremental economics** — `ΔNOPAT/ΔIC` over window vs WACC | income + balance | ≥ WACC + 10 pts | WACC to WACC+10 | < WACC |
| D | **Pricing power** — gross-margin level and 5-yr slope | `grossProfit/revenue` | level ≥ 40% **and** slope ≥ 0 | level 25–40% **or** slope ≥ 0 | level < 25% **and** slope < 0 |
| E | **Position** — reinvestment runway / share signal (segment growth, concentration in a growing segment) | `segments`, `latestQuarter.revenueYoY`, MD&A/transcript | leading & gaining in a growing segment | holding position | losing share / shrinking segment |

Test E is the only partly-qualitative one; it is a *tie-breaker*, never a primary
driver, and it must cite a figure (a segment's revenue and YoY, a share statement
in the MD&A) — it is not a free-text opinion like `1.md`'s sub-weights.

### 3.2 Combining rule (not a sum)

```
WIDE   if  A = Wide  AND  B = Wide  AND  D ≥ Narrow
           (excess returns are high, stable, and backed by pricing power)

NARROW if  not WIDE  AND  at least one of { A ≥ Narrow, C = Wide, D = Wide } holds
           (excess returns are present, or the reinvestment economics /
            pricing power are strong even if the average spread is thin)

NONE   otherwise
           (no evidence of durable excess returns)
```

### 3.3 Conflict-resolution clause (the acquisition case)

If a **structural break** was flagged (§2a) and the tests disagree — average
spread says `None` (A negative) but incremental economics and pricing power say
otherwise (C = Wide **and** D = Wide) — the engine resolves to **`Narrow`,
flagged `contingent`**. The label is `Narrow (contingent on acquired capital
earning its cost)`, and the report must state that the width rests on incremental
returns clearing the hurdle, not on the reported average spread. This is the
judgment a linear model cannot make: it is a *rule about which evidence to trust
when a corporate action distorts the denominator.*

## 4. Moat TREND — `Widening` / `Stable` / `Eroding` from slopes

Trend is the part a static scorecard misses entirely. It is the **sign and size
of the slope** of the moat evidence over the comparable window (use the last 3
comparable years to stay responsive; ordinary least squares or first-vs-last is
fine).

| Signal | Field basis | `Widening` | `Stable` | `Eroding` |
|--------|-------------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Spread slope | ROIC − WACC per year | ≥ +1.0 pt/yr | −1.0 to +1.0 | ≤ −1.0 pt/yr |
| Gross-margin slope | `grossProfit/revenue` per year | ≥ +0.5 pt/yr | flat | ≤ −0.5 pt/yr |
| Operating-margin slope | `operatingIncome/revenue` per year | rising | flat | falling |
| Incremental vs average ROIC | §1.1 vs §1 | incr > avg (capital improving) | ≈ equal | incr < avg (capital decaying) |

Combining rule:

```
WIDENING if ≥ 3 of 4 signals point up and none points sharply down
ERODING  if ≥ 2 of 4 signals point down (spread slope down counts double)
STABLE   otherwise
```

Trend is intentionally sensitive on the **downside** (spread-down double-counts):
the cost of missing an eroding moat — a value trap — is higher than the cost of
being slow to award a widening one.

## 5. How the moat modulates the five-bucket rating

This is the core claim of the document: the moat verdict **wraps** the live rule
in `conviction.ts` rather than adding a term to a sum. `computeConviction` and
`deriveLabel` are unchanged. Three structural levers sit around them.

### Lever 1 — Moat sets the minimum bear depth (moves `R` through `D`)

`conviction.ts` computes `bearDownside D = (price − bearImpliedPrice)/price` and
`R = E/D`. `desk.json` already enforces `bearFloor = 0.15` (the bear must sit
≥15% below price). The economic function of a moat is to **truncate the left tail
of intrinsic value** — a wide, durable moat keeps earning through a trough, so
the downside really is bounded; a company with no durable excess returns can
de-rate much further. So the moat sets a **moat-specific minimum bear depth**:

| Moat state | Minimum bear depth `D_floor` | Effect on `R` |
|------------|------------------------------|----------------|
| `Wide` + (`Widening`/`Stable`) | 0.15 (desk default) | shallowest defensible bear allowed → highest `R` |
| `Narrow` | 0.20 | modest floor |
| `None` | 0.30 | deep bear forced → `R` suppressed |
| any + `Eroding` | 0.35 | deepest floor |

The floor is a **minimum**, never a shave: the engine deepens a bear that is too
shallow for a thin moat, but it never shallows a bear the analyst already chose.
A wide moat *permits* a shallow bear; it does not *manufacture* one. This routes
the entire moat effect through the existing `D`, so `R` and `deriveLabel` do the
rest untouched.

### Lever 2 — Moat caps which bucket is reachable

```
STRONG BUY  requires  Width ≥ Narrow  AND  Trend = Widening
BUY         requires  Trend ≠ Eroding                 # no BUY into decay, however cheap
SELL / STRONG SELL    never blocked by the moat        # a thin moat must not stop a sell
```

The `Eroding → cap at HOLD` clause is the moat analogue of `2.md`'s hard veto,
but it is **earned from the slope of measured returns**, not from a static
leverage cutoff. It is the value-trap guard: a stock can screen cheap (`E` high)
precisely because its moat is decaying, and the engine refuses to call that a BUY.

### Lever 3 — Moat bounds the fair-value multiple the scenarios may defend

A wider, more durable moat justifies a longer competitive-advantage period, hence
a higher defensible exit multiple in the base/bull scenarios; an eroding moat
shortens it. The engine records `maxDefensibleMultiple = f(Width, Trend)` and the
scenario author must stay within it. The quantitative CAP/fade mechanics live in
`4.md` (reverse-DCF); this engine supplies the *fade rate* input:
`Widening → slow fade`, `Eroding → fast fade`.

### The wrapper (pseudocode)

```ts
// wraps computeConviction + deriveLabel; does not replace them
function moatModulatedLabel(
  scenarios: ScenarioIn[], price: number, desk: DeskRating, moat: MoatVerdict
): RatingLabel {
  const c = computeConviction(scenarios, price);           // E, D, R — unchanged

  // Lever 1: enforce the moat-specific minimum bear depth, recompute R
  const dFloor = bearFloorFor(moat);                       // 0.15 / 0.20 / 0.30 / 0.35
  const D = Math.max(c.bearDownside, dFloor);
  const R = D > 0 ? c.expectedUpside / D : null;

  let label = deriveLabel({ ...c, bearDownside: D, rewardRisk: R }, desk);

  // Lever 2: moat caps the reachable bucket
  if (label === "STRONG BUY" &&
      !(widthAtLeast(moat, "Narrow") && moat.trend === "Widening")) label = "BUY";
  if ((label === "STRONG BUY" || label === "BUY") &&
      moat.trend === "Eroding") label = "HOLD";

  return label;   // never more aggressive than deriveLabel; SELLs pass through
}
```

The wrapper only ever moves the label **toward HOLD or toward a sell**, never
more aggressive than the raw `(E, R)` rule — consistent with the
`conservativeNotch` discipline already in `conviction.ts`. The moat can *withhold*
conviction; it cannot invent it.

## 6. Why this is deeper than "moat 0–100 × 15%"

| Flat scorecard (`1.md` §4, `2.md`) | This engine |
|------------------------------------|-------------|
| Moat is a subjective 0–100, weight 15%, averaged into a sum | Moat is a measured spread series → two rule-based verdicts that gate, not add |
| One point in time | Level **and** slope over 5 years — a Trend axis a sum cannot represent |
| Blends away: a great growth score can hide a decaying moat | `Eroding` **caps** the bucket; no averaging past it |
| ROIC vs WACC is one 0–10 cell | ROIC vs WACC is the *substrate*; plus incremental ROIC and cash-ROIC cross-checks |
| Blind to stock-funded M&A distorting the denominator | Structural-break test + contingent-Narrow conflict rule |
| Moat and reward/risk live in separate weighted terms | Moat **reshapes** reward/risk through the bear floor — one economic channel |
| Hand-set sub-weights (Switching Costs 15%, …) nobody can source | Every cutoff is a threshold on a computed series with a named field |

The deepest difference: a linear model answers "how good is the moat?" with a
number. This engine answers three questions a number cannot — *is the excess
return real (Width), is it getting better or worse (Trend), and therefore how far
should the reward/risk rule be allowed to travel (modulation)?*

## 7. Worked example — AMD (`0000002488-26-000123.json`)

Price $504.20; market cap $822,148.52M; total debt $4,472M; five fiscal years
FY21–FY25.

### 7.1 ROIC series (t_eff = 15%)

```
InvestedCapital = totalDebt + totalEquity − cashAndInvestments
NOPAT           = operatingIncome × 0.85
```

| FY | Operating income | NOPAT | Debt + Equity − Cash | Invested capital | ROIC |
|----|-----------------:|------:|---------------------:|-----------------:|-----:|
| FY21 | 3,648 | 3,101 | 661 + 7,497 − 3,608 | 4,550 | **68.1%** |
| FY22 | 1,264 | 1,074 | 2,863 + 54,750 − 5,855 | 51,758 | **2.1%** |
| FY23 | 401 | 341 | 3,003 + 55,892 − 5,773 | 53,122 | **0.6%** |
| FY24 | 1,900 | 1,615 | 2,212 + 57,568 − 5,132 | 54,648 | **3.0%** |
| FY25 | 3,694 | 3,140 | 4,472 + 62,999 − 10,552 | 56,919 | **5.5%** |

**Structural break:** equity jumps $7,497M → $54,750M (+630%) FY21→FY22 —
the all-stock Xilinx acquisition. `break_FY22 = 47,208/4,550 = 10.4 > 0.50` →
flagged. FY21 is marked non-comparable (its 68% ROIC sits on a $4.6B capital base
that no longer exists). The comparable window is **FY22–FY25**.

### 7.2 WACC

```
wE = 822,148.52 / (822,148.52 + 4,472) = 0.9946
wD = 0.0054
ke = 4.3% + 1.7 × 4.5% = 11.95%
kd = 4.3% + 1.0% (interestCoverage 44× → investment grade) = 5.3%;  after-tax ≈ 4.5%
WACC = 0.9946 × 11.95% + 0.0054 × 4.5% = ~11.9%
```

AMD is ~99.5% equity-financed, so WACC ≈ cost of equity ≈ **11.9%**
(β, rf, ERP flagged as inputs per §1.2).

### 7.3 Spread and the corroborating series

| FY (comparable) | ROIC | Spread (ROIC − 11.9%) | Gross margin | Operating margin | FCF-ROIC |
|-----------------|-----:|----------------------:|-------------:|-----------------:|---------:|
| FY22 | 2.1% | −9.8 | 44.9% | 5.4% | 6.0% |
| FY23 | 0.6% | −11.3 | 46.1% | 1.8% | 2.1% |
| FY24 | 3.0% | −8.9 | 49.3% | 7.4% | 4.4% |
| FY25 | 5.5% | −6.4 | 49.5% | 10.7% | 11.8% |

**Incremental ROIC**, FY22→FY25: `ΔNOPAT/ΔIC = (3,140 − 1,074)/(56,919 − 51,758)
= 2,066 / 5,161 = 40.0%` — far above the 11.9% hurdle. From the FY23 trough it is
73.7%.

**Cash cross-check:** FY25 FCF-ROIC of 11.8% ≈ WACC 11.9% — on a *cash* basis AMD
is already at its hurdle, versus 5.5% on a NOPAT basis. The gap is the accounting
drag from Xilinx intangible amortization (§2b).

### 7.4 Width verdict

- Test A (level): median comparable spread ≈ −9.4 → **None**.
- Test B (stability): 0/4 comparable years with positive spread → **None**.
- Test C (incremental): 40.0% vs WACC+10 (21.9%) → **Wide**.
- Test D (pricing power): gross margin ~50%, slope +1.5 pt/yr → **Wide**.
- Test E (position): Data Center 48% of FY25 revenue, +107% YoY in Q2'26, stated
  server-share gains → leading & gaining in a growing segment → **Wide**.

The tests **conflict**: average spread says `None`, but incremental economics and
pricing power say `Wide`, and a structural break was flagged. The
conflict-resolution clause (§3.3) applies → **Width = `Narrow (contingent)`**.
The report must say plainly: AMD's moat rests on the capital deployed since
Xilinx earning ~40% incrementally and on ~50% gross margins, **not** on a reported
whole-company ROIC that still trails its cost of capital (5.5% vs 11.9%).

### 7.5 Trend verdict

- Spread slope FY23→FY25: −11.3 → −8.9 → −6.4 = **+2.45 pt/yr** → up.
- Gross-margin slope: 46.1% → 49.5% = **+1.1 pt/yr** → up.
- Operating-margin slope: 1.8% → 10.7% → up.
- Incremental ROIC (40%) >> average ROIC (5.5%) → capital improving → up.

Four of four up → **Trend = `Widening`**.

### 7.6 Rating modulation

AMD's live conviction (illustrative values consistent with the published rating,
where `R ≈ 0.37` and the label is **HOLD**): expected upside `E ≈ +15%`, and with
the analyst's actual deep bear `D ≈ 0.405` (a ~40% drawdown is credible for a
stock whose 52-week low is $149.85 against a $504.20 price), `R = 0.15/0.405 =
0.37`.

- **Lever 1 (bear floor):** Width = `Narrow` → `D_floor = 0.20`. The actual bear
  (0.405) is already deeper than the floor, so the floor does **not** bind; `D`
  and `R` are unchanged. The moat gives no basis to *shallow* the bear, because
  reported returns still trail WACC and the width is only contingent.
- **Lever 2 (bucket cap):** Trend = `Widening`, Width = `Narrow` → no cap applies
  (STRONG BUY would even be reachable on the moat; BUY is not blocked).
- **Result:** `deriveLabel` sees `E = +15%` (≥ +10%) but `R = 0.37` (< 0.5 BUY
  gate) → **HOLD**. The moat engine **confirms HOLD** and supplies the reason the
  raw rule cannot articulate: the long-term economics are improving (Widening,
  ~40% incremental ROIC, ~50% gross margin), but at $504 the reward/risk is too
  thin and the *reported* returns do not yet clear the cost of capital — so there
  is no warrant to shallow the bear into a BUY. This matches AMD's actual
  published HOLD.

**The levers doing real work (counterfactuals on the same `E = 15%`):**

- *If AMD's moat were `Wide` + durable* (reported ROIC clearing WACC with
  stability), Lever 1 would let the analyst defend a shallower bear, e.g.
  `D = 0.22`: `R = 0.15/0.22 = 0.68 ≥ 0.5` → **BUY**. A genuine wide moat
  legitimately unlocks the upgrade by bounding the downside.
- *If AMD's Trend were `Eroding`*, Lever 2 caps the label at **HOLD** even at
  `R = 0.68` — the value-trap guard refuses a BUY into decaying returns.

Same expected upside, three different ratings, driven entirely by the moat
verdict acting *through* the bear floor and the bucket cap — not by a weight in a
sum.

## 8. Limitations & required new data

**Required or wanted FactPack additions**

- **WACC inputs.** `rf`, `ERP`, and a per-name `beta` (or `sectorHurdle`) are the
  one genuinely external dependency. Until they exist the engine uses flagged
  defaults and a sector-beta table; the WACC used is recorded with every rating.
- **Effective tax expense / rate.** Needed to replace the assumed `t_eff` in
  NOPAT. The pack has neither pretax income nor tax expense, and `netIncome`
  cannot stand in (non-operating and tax items distort it).
- **Goodwill and intangibles.** The financing-side invested-capital definition
  cannot isolate goodwill, so the acquisition distortion (§2a) is detected but not
  *removed*; a `goodwill` row would let the engine also report an
  ex-goodwill ROIC directly instead of leaning on incremental ROIC as a proxy.
- **Interest expense.** Currently inferred from `ttm.interestCoverage`; a direct
  line would sharpen `kd` for leveraged names.

**Structural limitations**

- **Five years is short for persistence.** A true CAP can run 10–20 years;
  five points bound how much "durability" the statements can prove. The engine is
  honest about this — `Wide` requires stability *within the available window*, and
  a young issuer (few comparable years) should be capped at `Narrow` by data
  sufficiency, mirroring the young-issuer handling already in the pipeline.
- **Peer pricing signals are often absent.** In this very FactPack all four
  `peers[]` multiples are `null`, so cross-sectional pricing-power evidence is
  unavailable — the recurring "peerless peer" gap. Test E must then rest on the
  company's own segment series, and the cross-sectional read is left to `5.md`.
- **Banks and other non-industrial models.** For banks, `operatingIncome`,
  `totalDebt` and invested capital are not meaningful; the excess-return idea must
  be re-expressed as ROTCE − cost of equity (CET1-based), which this engine
  should branch to rather than force the industrial ROIC formula.
- **Intangible moats the statements never show.** Regulatory approvals, ecosystem
  lock-in, installed base, switching costs — the *causes* of the moat — are
  invisible to the numbers except through the excess-return *evidence* they leave.
  The engine deliberately scores the evidence, not the story, and flags width as
  `contingent` whenever the evidence is thin or distorted.
- **WACC is a proxy, and the spread's sign can hinge on it.** AMD's FY25 spread is
  −6.4 pts at WACC 11.9%; at a 10% WACC it is −4.5, at 14% it is −8.5. The verdict
  should always be reported with the WACC used and a one-line sensitivity, so a
  `Narrow`/`None` boundary is never presented as more precise than the input.
