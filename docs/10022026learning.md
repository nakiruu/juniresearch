# Learnings — report pipeline and review loop (October 2–3, 2026)

What this run of analyses taught us about producing Juniper reports. Covers the reports published or
re-published on October 2–3, 2026: RDVT, GDYN, ACLS, ESAB, FOUR, PGY, DSP, VRTX, PLOW, LQDT, EXFY, CVLG,
MQ, QLYS, KRYS, MNST, FTI, THG, EVER, GNK and AMSC.

## 1. Process that worked

- **Author and reviewer are separate agents.** The author writes `data/judgment/<T>/<A>.json`, builds with
  `--skip-review`, and renders `synth:review-brief`. A different agent reviews by following the brief and
  writes only `.editorial.json`. Fixes go back to the same author; re-checks go to the same reviewer
  (warm delta brief).
- **Nothing reaches `main` unreviewed.** Drafts are committed on the dev branch as
  `wip(<T>): … (unreviewed; not for main)`. Because a merge carries every file on the branch, an approved
  report waits until every other draft on the branch is approved too (FTI waited for THG; GNK for AMSC).
- **Publish = gated build + `--no-ff` merge.** Run `synth:build` without `--skip-review` (it must pass the
  editorial gate), commit, merge into `main`, push, then fast-forward the dev branch to `main`.
- **The review round caps at 2 in the schema.** Further re-checks stay "round 2". A Minor that is still
  open after the final re-check is declined with a note ("deferred to the next synthesis") rather than
  fixed, because editing the judgment after the last review would leave it unreviewed.
- **Re-publishing a report on corrected inputs** (FOUR, DSP) keeps the original report date
  (`synth:build --date YYYY-MM-DD`) and gets a fresh reviewer on a `--full-brief`.
- **If the author agent is lost** (e.g. a container restart), start a new author for the fix round and
  point it at the prompt, the current judgment and the findings file.

## 2. House lessons for authors

Reviewers flagged these on nearly every report. Put them in the author prompt up front.

**Grounding**
- Every figure verbatim from the Facts/Context blocks. Never compute a new number; describe it in words.
- Don't recall anything that is not in the prompt (tenures, product details, peers, causes, charter rates).
- When the company explains a result, use its explanation. Don't substitute your own driver story
  (THG: the report blamed catastrophes and reserves when the company credited current-year improvement).
- Keep the source's hedges ("may", "could", "may be deemed", "partially fund").
- Quote a phrase with the subject it describes (EVER: "substantial majority" was about referrals, not revenue).
- Don't present a vendor metric as "our" definition.

**Dating and periods**
- Date stale facts: proxy items, 10-K items, guidance, backlog, dividend actions.
- Say whether a comparison is sequential or year over year.
- Use the company's own period labels. For off-calendar fiscal years, describe vendor-labelled
  quarters by their end date.
- Check which window a trailing multiple covers before explaining it (GNK: the FY25 loss sat outside the
  trailing twelve months; AMSC: the trailing P/E is not FY-based).

**Structure**
- Argue each claim once; other sections point back in words. The most repeated offenders were customer
  concentration, control, the EPS dip and recent price moves.
- State upgrade/downgrade triggers once, in the final recommendation, and never a trigger the current
  data already meets (THG).
- Every bullish section needs a real counter-case, and every paragraph must reach a view.
- Avoid tics: "The counter-case:", "Our view:", repeated "Watch", repeated "the sources do not…".

**Valuation**
- Anchor Base and Bear to a stated multiple, EPS, book value or Street figure a reader can check, and say
  the basis. A vendor "NTM" multiple is often really a current-year multiple (EVER, AMSC).
- The Bear case must be a real downside, and the weights must match what the data shows. Acknowledge
  sharp recent price moves in the price history, even when no source explains them (EVER).
- Weights must say what the author believes. Don't park an outcome in Bear to make the result
  "conservative" (AMSC, F-22). If a call is close, argue the deciding split from both sides and say it
  is close (AMSC, F-24).
- Don't tune prices or weights to land on either side of the BUY line. Whatever label the rule derives
  is the call. A one-notch-conservative call is allowed only if the report says so.

**Governance**
- Use the proxy excerpt only, and date it. Attribute stakes to the holder the footnote names (EVER: the
  Blundin-affiliated group, not Link Ventures alone).
- A stake under 50% is not "control". Give a view on what the ownership means for outside holders.

## 4. Rating outcomes worth remembering

- **Draft labels moved in review.** EVER (BUY → HOLD), AMSC (BUY → HOLD) and KRYS (BUY → HOLD) were
  narrow BUYs that did not survive honest weighting. Treat a reward/risk just over 0.5× as fragile.
- **Corrected inputs changed one label.** FOUR fell to HOLD once FCF was corrected. DSP's label held
  because its scenarios never used FCF, though its conviction score fell from 84 to 74.

## 5. Housekeeping rules carried forward

- Ask the owner before changing anything involving a lock.
- Never ask for tokens or secrets, and don't use the Schwab token from the cloud.
- Don't trade or reconcile from the cloud.
- No model IDs in commits or PRs, and no PRs unless asked.
- Don't put the owner's email in outbound requests (SEC User-Agent included) unless asked.
- When removing a ticker, also clear its `seen.json` entry and every file associated with it.

## 6. Added 2026-10-03 (later): agents, data sources and the sleeve research

**Running agents**
- A usage-limit 429 kills background agents mid-task. After the limit resets, resume each one with
  `SendMessage` ("continue from where you stopped"). It keeps its transcript, so finished work isn't
  repeated.
- A container restart loses running agents. Start a fresh author for the fix round, and point it at
  the prompt, the current judgment and `editorial.json`.
- Have an adversarial critic **re-run the numbers**, not only read the plan. On the sleeve plan this
  caught a design that was over-engineered, a sizing formula that subtracted the T-bill weight twice,
  and a misread of the doctrine.
- Pre-register tie-breaks and pass bars before testing. Example: "if the simpler rule wins in both
  halves, adopt it." Then follow them.

**Data sources** (sleeve research, reports A–F in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-03-sleeve-research/`)
- **FRED CSV** (`fredgraph.csv?id=…`) works without a key, with two limits:
  - HY OAS (`BAMLH0A0HYM2`) returns only the last 3 years. Log it daily if it will ever be needed.
  - `SP500` returns only the last 10 years. Use Yahoo `^GSPC` or `^SP500TR` instead.
  - `BAA10Y` goes back to 1986.
- **Yahoo chart API**:
  - Use `query2`, because `query1` returns 429 on bursts.
  - Use `period1`/`period2` with `interval=1d`; `range=max` silently returns monthly bars.
  - Send no email in the User-Agent.
- **Shibui**:
  - Has SPY, IWM, IWO, IWF, HYG and JNK, but prices are not adjusted for dividends.
  - Has no QUAL, MTUM, USMV or VIX products, and no indices.
  - Covers only names listed today, so breadth and backtests are survivor-biased.
- **SEC tickers**: an unknown ticker is often a typo (ASMC was AMSC). Check
  `company_tickers.json` and ask the owner.

**Sleeve findings** (plan: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-03-dynamic-sleeve-plan.md`, rev 2, plan only)
- Rotating between growth and core equity sleeves does **not** reduce drawdowns: both fell about
  51%. No signal predicted the growth-minus-core spread. Only a T-bill leg cut drawdowns, from about
  −52% to about −40%.
- A two-state trend rule (S&P total return vs its 10-month average) matched or beat a graded
  3-signal rule, with about half the switches. Volatility and credit are context only, and a credit
  risk-off signal was followed by *higher* next-month returns.
- Expected trade-off:
  - about 7–10 pp less drawdown in slow bears;
  - almost nothing in V-shaped or choppy markets;
  - about 0.2–1.5 pp a year of return given up.
- Engine facts that any overlay must respect:
  - The turnover breaker is off in production.
  - `clipToTurnover` halts any plan that contains a sell.
  - The 2.5 pp per-name trade band would turn a sleeve shift into a no-op.
  - The sector cap applies within each `allocateCapped` call, not across them.
  - An ETF with no report is frozen as NO_SIGNAL.
  - A held name downgraded to HOLD is **exited** unless it is locked.

## 7. Added 2026-10-06: KNSA, CALX, CSCO, AEIS

- **Check capture notes against the rendered prompt.** On the free path the prompt says "peers pending a peer data source" even when FMP peers were saved, so a note about the "vendor peer list" made authors describe peers that do not exist (Critical findings on CALX, CSCO, AEIS). Never name a fact in a note (e.g. "commercial paper") that is not in the prompt; authors will cite it.
- **Verify debt and cash against the filing's balance sheet** before writing, on every free-path pack.
- **Run the full vitest suite before every merge to `main`.**
- **A Bear weight must be argued on the Bear case itself** (AEIS F-20); an outcome outside every scenario is a tail, not a reason to raise the Bear weight.

## 8. Added 2026-10-06 (later): NET

- **NET published as HOLD $330–380** (E −2.2%, D −55.3%). It is close to SELL: swapping the Bull and Bear weights, or anchoring Base to the mean target, crosses −5%. Two review rounds found 29 findings (2 Critical); all were fixed.
- **The capture-note trap applies to the orchestrator's own author brief.** The brief explained the FCF gap by naming "capitalized internal-use software", which was not in the prompt, and the author cited it. Describe a prompt row's definition only in terms of figures the prompt shows.
- **After any debt or cash fix, check the gate outcome.** Capturing NET's debt made it a cash-only "net debtor" with negative coverage, and that triggered a WEAK zone (HOLD ceiling, −10 conviction) until the gate was changed.
- **The prompt never labels the P/S window.** Don't call it trailing-twelve-month.

## 9. Added 2026-10-06 (later): ADI

- **ADI published as HOLD $400–470** (E −0.8%, D −46.9%; Bull $533 / Base $463.50 / Bear $223.47 at 20/55/25). The draft was HOLD at +6.4% with a Bear of $331 that held EPS flat while its story said the cycle turns (F-1). Pricing the turn the report called its main risk moved E near the SELL line. Two review rounds produced 31 findings, no Critical; 30 were fixed and one Minor was deferred.
- **A SELL price trigger must sit where the page's own math flips the label** (F-25). With a weighted value near $417, SELL derives around the 52-week high, not above the target range.
- **A blank TTM ratio is a pipeline signal, not a data gap.** ADI's null net debt/EBITDA traced to the YTD bug above.

## 10. Added 2026-10-06 (later): BSX

- **BSX published as HOLD $40–47** (E +5.5%, D −21.5%, R 0.26×; Bull $60.00 / Base $44.20 / Bear $33.17 at 20/55/25). It is close: moving about ten points of weight from Bear to Bull derives BUY, and the report labels the split "a judgment, not a finding". Two review rounds produced 26 findings, no Critical; 25 were fixed and one Minor was deferred.
- **An unexplained price collapse is not a split.** BSX fell from $105.65 to about $42 with an unchanged share count. Confirm there are no split events and that shares are stable before treating a price as an error.
- **The free path's "single reportable segment" line can contradict the MD&A** (BSX reports MedSurg and Cardiovascular). Tell the author to treat it as a source disagreement and follow the MD&A.
- **A reason for a weighting must come from a source** (F-21). The Q3 outlook was cited as evidence that pressures persisted, though no document gives a reason for the step-down.
- **An assumed multiple with nothing on the page is not an anchor** (F-3). Re-anchor to page figures, and check that "lower multiple" holds on the same EPS basis.

## 11. Added 2026-10-06 (later): VST

- **VST published as HOLD $148–178** (E +5.6%, D −34.0%, R 0.16×; Bull $210.25 / Base $174.05 / Bear $106.00 at 25/55/20). The label rests on the October 6 close of $160.50, which followed a sharp two-session rise; most earlier closes derive BUY on the same scenarios, and the report says so. Two review rounds produced 22 findings, no Critical; 19 were fixed. Three Minors were declined: one because the build requires the segment entry to be named "Consolidated", and two deferred to the next synthesis.
- **Confirm a big report-day move against the official close before building.** Yahoo's `regularMarketTime` showed $160.50 as the October 6 close. Shibui lags a day, so its input-check warning ($145 vs $161) is expected on a report-day move and is not a reason to change the price.
- **When the label depends on the day's close, the commentary must say so** (F-1). Say what the earlier closes would derive and that the Base inherited a move no source explains.
- **A concept-order fix for one filer can break another.** The BSX lease-line fallback cut VST's debt. After any change to concept order, re-run the debt check on every pack verified earlier that day.
- **The stored label does not move with the price.** A HOLD stays a HOLD in the trade engine until the report is re-run, even if the price falls into BUY territory. That is why the advisory re-label flag was built.
